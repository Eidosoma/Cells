## V. Text under which an honest engine could not pass, or a wrong table could

### V1. `J0` (READOUTS §3, SPEC §4, amendment 1 item 5)

**The gap.** `J0` runs at one step, so it can form no class-S entry, and READOUTS gives `n_T_*` and `H_tot` class S.

**The ruling.** `J0` has no class-S entry. `n_T_A`, `n_T_B`, `H_face` and `H_tot` (which is `H_face` there) are judged by
its E twin, by the R sums and by arm 6's R energy identity. The references publish no entry of `J0` for those four, as
for `F7`'s three (amendment 1, item 3). `n_T_K`, `V_K` and the router heats stay class E, and `n_U_*` class R.

**Can a verdict depend on it? Yes:** an entry that cannot be formed is not a pass.

### V2. `F6 h=4.0 refused` (CRITERIA §4 and §8, TABLE §3)

**The gap.** A run refused at parse has no parsed definition, no ID the engine ran and no engine record, and CRITERIA
§4's "**A refused run cannot pass.** With no failing entry it is at best inconclusive, and so is its arm" had no exception
for it.

**The ruling.** Its `inputs` and `definition` are the submitted declaration, its `solver`, `heat_id` and `operators`
those declared, its `record` the driver's own file, and its `largest_ion_change_fmol` 0.0. **The expected refusal
counts only as CRITERIA §3.7 says,** and is neither refused nor inconclusive under §4 and §8, (2) with one exception: a
missing run, one with a departing echo, or one with a provenance fault counts as refused another way, so arm 3 is at
best inconclusive.

**Can a verdict depend on it? Yes:** arm 3 would have been inconclusive on the refusal it expects, and without the
exception it would have passed on a run that was absent or not the registered one.

### V3. A retry run is registered only when owed (amendment 4, item 2)

**The gap.** Amendment 4 registered every retry run outright, so when none is owed they are missing and their arms
inconclusive.

**The ruling.**
- A retry run is registered when it is **owed:** an entry of its world (and heat) is inconclusive by order, V7's
  included, by amendment 4's naming rule. The judge's first pass writes `retry_owed.json` (layout in L2). The lead
  records its sha256 before any retry run starts, and the builder runs exactly those.
- **"Missing" is decided over all of a phase's tables together.**
- A retry run that is present and not owed is recorded, flagged and not judged.

**Can a verdict depend on it? Yes:** every arm with an S or L entry was at best inconclusive.

**The ruling.**

- (2) If that repeated rehearsal misses a bound, the entries that rest on it are recorded and not judged, and arm 6 is at

**Can a verdict depend on it? Yes:** FR-57's third line, through a bound that could be met nowhere, and through what a

### V5. Arm 7 (CRITERIA §3.8, amendment 4 item 1)

**The gap.** The map named records by the wash's own names, so it could leave out what matters, and arm 7 could pass with
little judged.

**The ruling** (layouts in L1).
- **The map is keyed by meaning.** Eight are required of every pair: `latest_sample`, `latest_supply`, `latest_heat`,
  `cumulative_sample`, `cumulative_supply`, `cumulative_heat`, `cumulative_sample_free_energy` and
  `cumulative_buffer_free_energy`; and `label_removals` for `labelled twins True` alone (2: the one labelled pair; `labelled
  twins False` is its unlabelled twin, and no other pair has labels). "Per event" in §3.8 means the latest-event records,
  compared on every saved row. The builder may add meanings, compared the same way. A required meaning that the map does
  not give, or whose `mapped:<meaning>` is absent from either side's `digests`, refuses the pair.
- **`both_differ`** is the closed list of arrays both sides save under one name that legitimately differ, and so are
  exempt from §3.8's equal digests: identity metadata only (an ID, a fingerprint, a wall time), each with its reason, and
  never state, observation rows, ledgers or diagnostics.
- **`wash_only`: one disclosed exception.** Amendment 4's "A record of the wash is never in `wash_only`" does not hold
  for the one record that keeps a magnitude of the wash's energy bookings, a rounding scale and not physics. It may be
  listed there with its reason. (3) Each entry there carries its reason and `record`: true for an amount, a heat, a free
  energy or that magnitude, and false for a count, a control or a ledger's error. At most one is true, the magnitude's,
  and its array is no required meaning's `wash`. The judge refuses a pair whose `wash_only` is otherwise, and the checker
  confirms every mark. So a record of the wash other than the magnitude is mapped, or its pair is refused, and entries
  marked false have no limit.
- **Each pair is bound by a hash** of the wash side's whole `definition`, equal to the strings note's (R2), and all six
  differ. The two sides' shared parts, each `definition` with that side's subtrees at `definition_paths` removed, hash
  alike. The canonical form is L1's. In arm 7, `definition` is the declaration the run was given, before any default is
  added.
- (2) **Where the hashes come from.** The pairs' wash-side declarations are committed as data by the builder, from the
  fixtures by a committed script (`takeover_pairs.json`, `{"<pair>": <definition>}`, the rehearsal's pair too). At the
  registration tag the lead regenerates them from the fixtures, compares, and writes the note's hashes from that. So
  "never from a table" (R2) holds.
- **No two runs of a phase's tables share a `record_sha256`:** runs that do are refused, their readouts are judged, and a
  fail stands. The checker compares the names in `digests` with the arrays of the record file. (2) It also recomputes the
  digests of one pair from its two record files, all six if cheap, and confirms every `both_differ`, `wash_only` and
  `router_only` entry and its reason, and (3) every mark: the magnitude's alone is true.
- **Arm 7 is rehearsed on one invented pair,** the builder's: a wash fixture that is none of the six. Three planted copies
  of its entries, which the judge forms, must be scored as the text says: a changed digest and a changed mapped record
  fail, and a missing router run is refused.
- **When.** The map's content, and `takeover_pairs.json`, are filed before the registration tag (the rehearsal's before the
  rehearsal). Before the judge's tag only the layouts and a meaningless sample are needed. This replaces "filed before the
  judge's tag".

**Can a verdict depend on it? Yes:** arm 7, and so FR-57.

### V6. M1's format (CRITERIA §3.7, TABLE §3)

**The gap.** "The word `residual` and then a number" does not say what is searched, or where a unit stands.

**The ruling.** One format, found anywhere in the message:
`<ledger> ledger: residual <num> <unit>, tolerance <num> <unit>`, with one space at each place shown. `<ledger>` is a name
in the strings note, and both `<unit>`s are its unit there. `<num>` is an optional sign, digits with an optional point
and fraction, and an optional exponent. At least one occurrence is present, and every `<ledger> ledger:` with a name in
the note is followed by the rest. (2) `<ledger>` is not preceded by a letter, a digit or an underscore, and the exponent
letter is `e` or `E`. On P8's stops and on both expected stops one of them is the energy ledger, and on P8's
M2 reads its numbers. §3.7's |residual| > tolerance > 0 stands for every one.

**Can a verdict depend on it? Yes:** M1, M2 and P18.

### V7. An order read from differences, one of which is at rounding (CRITERIA §3.5, amendment 4 item 2)

**The gap.** The order is unresolved where either difference is at most 10 e_round, and τ alone then decides. A run
copied from another step has a zero difference, so it passed.

**The ruling.** The order is unresolved only where **both** d₁ and d₂ are at most 10 e_round. Where exactly one is, the
entry is inconclusive by order, whatever its error. (3) **Above τ** the precedent holds (FR-25's, D278 and D282, as FR-38's
second comparison has it in its CRITERIA §4): an entry fails where its order is inside the window or unresolved. A
resolved order outside the window means that the estimate is not trusted, so the entry is inconclusive by order and owed
the retry. After the retry the same rule applies to the finer triple, and an entry still outside stays inconclusive, never
a pass. A step that a retry drops is reported as inconclusive and is not claimed: the verdict states the steps its pass
covers, everything else judged on the dropped run (R, E, B, N, D) still counts, and the arm is not kept inconclusive by
it, or the retry would have no use. That a wrong coarse run is forgiven this way is among what the comparison cannot
show (D).

**Can a verdict depend on it? Yes.** It is the one change to how an order reads, and an honest entry's differences lie
far above e_round by this author's estimate.

**The gap.** No link touches S, so the twins could compare two runs in which nothing binds, and pass as equal.

**The ruling.**

  the sites. The count is this author's derivation, and the rehearsal shows it on `inv-F7` (2).
- **`bound_L_S` at the last output is above 10 e_round in both twins** (2; this item's e_round, at that output). Otherwise
  the twin's E entry is not formed and its run is inconclusive: nothing passes empty. `F7 h=1.0` owns both twins' entries
  (C8). How much and how fast `F7` binds is not judged.
- **SPEC §1's Why for `F7`** reads: a node no link touches is left alone, its binding included.
- **SPEC §8's invented set** also holds a world with a site node and its no-router twin. It already has them.

**Can a verdict depend on it? Yes:** arm 3, and what a pass of it says.

## L. Layouts

**L1. `takeover_map.json`,** version 2, and `takeover_map_rehearsal.json` for the rehearsal's pair:

```json
{"kind": "liquid-routing takeover map, version 2",
 "pairs": {"<pair>": {
   "definition_paths": {"wash": ["<JSON pointer>"], "router": ["<JSON pointer>"]},
   "records": {"<meaning>": {"wash": "<array>", "router": "<array>", "index": null}},
   "both_differ": {"<array>": "<reason>"},
   "wash_only": {"<array>": {"reason": "<reason>", "record": false}}, "router_only": {"<array>": "<reason>"}}}}
```

- `index` has one item per axis of the router's array (an integer selects there, null keeps), or is null for the whole. Each
  side's `digests` holds `mapped:<meaning>`, the selected part in the wash record's shape and type, and the two are equal.
  Every other array that one side alone saves is in `wash_only`, with its reason and its mark (3, V5), or in `router_only`
  with its reason.
- **Pointers** (2) are side-specific. A pointer names a subtree of its own side's `definition` and is removed from that side
  alone; one that does not resolve there refuses the pair. It is never the empty pointer (the whole document), which refuses
  the pair too, and never a whole list that holds more than the wash's or the router's own entries. The checker confirms the
  last, since the judge cannot read it.
- **Canonical form.** JSON of the object with keys sorted, separators `,` and `:`, UTF-8 with no escaping of non-ASCII, no
  NaN; every number that is not a boolean written as `repr(float(x))`; the sha256 of its bytes. Pointers are resolved
