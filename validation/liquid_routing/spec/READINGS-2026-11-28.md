## 1. The lead's two rulings on the judge's readings

### J10, OFF: V7 does not reach CRITERIA §3.4's pairs

**The reading the judge had on** (its switch `L_PAIRS_TAKE_V7`): V7's case of exactly one difference at rounding is read
into §3.4's L pairs, so that a pair with exactly one error at most 10 e_round makes T2's or T4's order entry inconclusive
by order.

**The ruling: the text as written.** CRITERIA §3.4 (L78–80, and L84 for T4): a pair is resolved where e_j and e_{j+1}
both exceed 10 e_round; a resolved pair's order outside [2.5, 3.5] makes the entry inconclusive; "An unresolved pair's
order is reported, not judged." A pair with either error at most 10 e_round is unresolved.

**Why.**
- **V7 names §3.5 alone:** its heading, "(CRITERIA §3.5, amendment 4 item 2)" (amendment 5, L137), and its row in that
  amendment's replacement table, "CRITERIA §3.5's order bullet, its sentence on 'either'" (L364).
- **V7's rule is written in §3.5's terms:** d₁, d₂ and τ (L142–143). §3.4 has e_j and no τ, so "an entry at most τ"
  has nothing to read there.
- **V7's gap does not arise in §3.4.** The gap was a run copied from another step, which passed because τ alone decided
  (L139–140). In §3.4 nothing passes by its order alone: each step's transfer is judged by class B against its own
  closed form (§3.3, L73; C9, L206, the default steps too), and each default heat by class R and class B (§3.4, L82–83).
  A step copied from another fails there.

**What it changes.** It is the one ruling of the two that removes an inconclusive: the switch-on reading would make an
honest entry inconclusive where its finest pair has one error at rounding, which amendment 2 item 5 (L60–63) says may
happen at `eq-hi` and `r4-hi`. It softens no fail: a resolved pair outside its window still makes its entry inconclusive,
and every class B and R entry is judged as before. It is looser than the switch-on reading, and not than the text, and it
rests on the lines cited alone.

### J12, KEPT: the event term enters every e_round whose path crosses an event

**The reading.** CRITERIA §1's event term, 8 u s once, is added to every e_round whose path crosses an exchange: the rows
after it (the sum, as amendment 2 item 1 says, the exact heat identity, and the first step after it), class S's e_round
where the last compared time is after it, and the exchange's `sample` and `heat` by class S. It is not added to `before`,
which is class E (R9, L191), nor to the exchange's R entries, whose bounds §1 states in full (8 u N_x and 32 u s_F, L30).

**Its scope (the lead's ruling).** Every event in TABLE's sense, an exchange or one of arm 4's deliveries (TABLE §3,
L45), adds 8 u s once to the e_round of every quantity whose path crosses it: CRITERIA §1's plain words (L17), for each
event.

**Why.**
- CRITERIA §1, L17: "e_round = m (S + 1) u s. An event adds 8 u s, once." The term is stated with the formula, not with
  one quantity.
- Amendment 2 item 1 (L23–24) gives the sum "CRITERIA §1's arm 2 row with its event term, unchanged": it names a term
  that exists, and does not create one for the sum alone.
- LAWS §9 (L173) reads with it: "An event costs no more than 8 roundings, so it never adds to a path longer than a
  step's." The event leaves a step's count m as it is, and §1 adds its own 8 roundings once, to every quantity whose path
  crosses it. Read alone, L173 could be taken to add nothing; CRITERIA, which holds the tolerances (README, L43), adds
  the term in §1 (L17), and amendment 2 item 1 uses it. LAWS §9's next sentence, which puts a portion's amount inside the
  same counts (L174), leaves m as it is in the same way.

**What it changes.** It is looser than reading the term into the sum alone, by the term itself: 8 u s, the text's own
number, fixed before any run. For each event, it is looser than once for a run by 8 u s for each delivery after the first:
at most 16 u s in a registered world, since `G3` and `G4` hold three deliveries each (`inputs.json`), against e_round's
m (S + 1) u s. It changes no count.
