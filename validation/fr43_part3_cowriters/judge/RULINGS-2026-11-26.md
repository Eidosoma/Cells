**How a refusal acts** (CRITERIA.md:43-48, 147-150, 180, 321, 344-350, 425-426):
- A run refused by a key, a leaf or its provenance leaves its entries unformed, so it cannot pass. What can be compared is
  compared all the same, and a miss fails. Its family is inconclusive at best; a `judged` run refused with kind `check`
  fails.
- Where the echo or the table's hash departs, nothing of the run is judged. Its family is inconclusive at best, and a miss
  in it goes unjudged.
- Plants act on the arm. An arm fails if a family fails or a plant is not caught, and is inconclusive if a family is
  inconclusive or a plant is unscorable.

So a key refusal is stricter than a flag, and is also stricter than an echo departure, which leaves misses unjudged. A plant
"not caught" is stricter than "unscorable".
