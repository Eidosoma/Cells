## Eidosoma research brief — November 13, 2026

A particularly strong week for reconstructibility: writable collective memory, experimentally validated planarian organizers, reversible fate programs and lineage-specific recovery after reprogramming. Direct voltage-pattern control remains thin, but body-agent communication is unusually well represented.

### 1. [Associative Learning and Memory Erasure in a Synthetic Multicellular System](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.09.765282)

**Preprint — November 12**

- **Genuinely new:** Five engineered *E. coli* strains divide coincidence detection, memory maintenance, conditional readout, response and erasure among themselves. A shared quorum-sensing molecule serves simultaneously as the communication channel and extracellular memory trace. The consortium retains an association after both stimuli are removed and can erase it on command.
- **Why it matters:** This is an exceptionally clear implementation of plastic heredity at the collective level: history changes future response, the record exists outside any single member, and writing, reading and erasure are separately controllable.
- **Evidence boundary:** The conditioning behavior is experimentally demonstrated, but it is an engineered state machine—not evidence for spontaneous cognition or general learning in bacterial communities.
- **Verdict:** **Deep read #1.** Likely useful both as a citation and as an architecture to reproduce computationally.

### 2. [Canonical Wnt signaling controls proliferation and tissue differentiation during planarian whole-body regeneration](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.12.766096)

**Preprint — November 13**

- **Genuinely new:** Grafted anterior or posterior planarian tips induce patterned heads or tails, experimentally establishing their organizer activity. Manipulating canonical Wnt signaling changes early stem-cell proliferation and the later differentiation of neurons, muscle and parenchymal cells; some regeneration can be rescued in irradiated animals.
- **Why it matters:** This connects a spatial organizer to both growth and coordinated reconstruction of several tissue classes. It is close to the question of how a small anatomical region communicates a larger target state to distributed cellular workers.
- **Evidence boundary:** Organizer function and Wnt dependence are demonstrated. The experiments do not establish that Wnt alone encodes the complete target anatomy; other positional systems almost certainly participate.
- **Verdict:** **Deep read #2**, especially for the organizer and whole-body coordination sections of *How Patterns Return*.

### 3. [Cell adhesion molecule–related proteins promote cytoneme elongation from Sonic Hedgehog-producing cells](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aei4501)

**Science Advances — November 13**

- **Genuinely new:** BOC and CDON—normally described as adhesion-related proteins—recruit FARP1 and Cdc42 to build long SHH-carrying cytonemes. Removing them reduces protrusion-localized Cdc42 activity and cytoneme formation; live imaging in mouse spinal cords links CDON loss to altered neuronal fate specification.
- **Why it matters:** This provides molecular machinery for directed, contact-like morphogen communication rather than simple undirected diffusion. Cells actively construct the channels through which patterning information travels.
- **Evidence boundary:** Cytoneme formation and its developmental consequences are experimentally supported. The paper does not show regeneration, memory or an explicit anatomical set point.
- **Verdict:** **Deep communication-mechanism read.** Relevant to adding explicit signaling protrusions to future Cells models.

### 4. [Reversible expression of a cartilage “master regulator” during axolotl limb development and regeneration](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.10.765670)

**Preprint — November 12**

- **Genuinely new:** Cells expressing `Sox9` during development do not all become cartilage. Cells that later silence it adopt fibroblast-like identities and, unexpectedly, contribute more extensively to skeletal regeneration—restarting `Sox9` when new cartilage is required.
- **Why it matters:** This is strong evidence that transient access to a developmental program can remain functionally reusable after the overt state disappears. Current identity, developmental history and regenerative competence are therefore separable.
- **Evidence boundary:** Fate mapping demonstrates reuse of the program, but it does not identify how the earlier `Sox9` episode is remembered or how cells determine that cartilage is missing.
- **Verdict:** **Deep read #3.** Very close to Eidosoma’s acquired-record and reversible-commitment arguments.

### 5. [Partial reprogramming uncouples rejuvenation, function and recoverability in human cells](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.09.764947)

**Preprint — November 12**

- **Genuinely new:** Fibroblasts, T cells and endothelial cells respond very differently to reversible OSKM reprogramming. Reduced methylation age does not reliably predict restored identity, proliferation or function; endothelial cells recover particularly well, whereas fibroblasts retain remodeling abnormalities.
- **Why it matters:** This sharply supports Eidosoma’s distinction between appearing younger, recovering a viable state and returning to functional identity. “Return” is multidimensional and cannot be summarized by one clock or distance metric.
- **Evidence boundary:** The lineage-specific recovery differences and functional assays are demonstrated. Reduced methylation age is not proof of organismal rejuvenation, and longer-term safety remains unknown.
- **Verdict:** **Deep conceptual read**, and directly relevant to evaluation criteria in *How Patterns Return*.

### 6. [Tissue-like viscoelasticity drives muscle stem-cell expansion through mechanical memory](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.06.764681)

**Preprint — November 9**

- **Genuinely new:** Muscle-like, stress-relaxing hydrogels accelerate muscle stem-cell activation and expansion while producing persistent stem-like behavior through focal-adhesion stabilization and Rac–Akt signaling.
- **Why it matters:** A transient physical environment writes a durable intracellular response state. This complements last week’s geometric-memory result by showing how tissue mechanics can become a retained cellular record relevant to regenerative competence.
- **Evidence boundary:** Persistent mechanical conditioning is demonstrated ex vivo; improved long-term muscle regeneration in vivo has not yet been established.
- **Verdict:** **Focused deep read** for mechanical records and extracellular-state coupling.

**Reading order:** synthetic collective memory → planarian organizers → reversible `Sox9` competence → partial-reprogramming recoverability. The first and fourth items are especially useful for the next revision of *How Patterns Return*.
