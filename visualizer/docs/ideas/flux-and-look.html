Flux in the cell, and a better look at rest

Options for a flux mode in the 3D Cell view, off by default, and for a richer look when nothing flows. Every sketch is live: it is drawn on your device, from invented numbers, and shows its own frame rate. Nothing here is built.

  1. How flux is drawn six styles in one sketch. I would take beads, with trails for the bold strength.
  2. How time is treated a bead that stands for many ions, and a slow-motion factor you set.
  3. How flux is switched on nothing moves at rest; a part's flux shows when you select it.
  4. Where the numbers come from first, the looping run the engine makes of each species' cell.
  5. The resting look ten effects to switch on and off. Five cost little and add most.

1How flux is drawn

A piece of membrane with a sodium channel, a potassium channel, a chloride channel in balance, and a pump that carries three sodium out for two potassium in. Switch between six ways of showing what passes. Drag to turn.

StyleReads wellFailsCost to draw
Beads My pickYou can count them, so the legend can say what one stands for. Direction is plain. Works in a still picture.Hundreds of channels at once become a hail. Needs one stream per kind of channel, not per channel.Light. A few hundred instanced spheres.
Trails Bold strengthBeads with a tail: direction shows even at a glance, and it is the most striking with bloom.A tail's length reads as speed, which the engine does not have. Keep every tail the same length.Light to moderate. About six times the instances, plus bloom.
RibbonsWidth is the rate, as in a flow chart. Calm, and exact at a distance.Looks like a pipe of fluid. The moving stripes carry direction only; their speed means nothing and may be read as if it did.Light. One tube per stream.
PulsesMany channels at once without clutter: each one blinks at its own rate.Two blink rates are hard to compare. Blinking tires. Needs a still form for reduced motion.Light.
SwarmThe truest picture: ions jostle at random on both sides, most never cross, and a few drift through. Looks alive.The net rate is hard to read off. Thousands of dots everywhere would swamp the cell. Best for one channel in close-up.Moderate. Thousands of points moved on the graphics card.
LightNo tokens at all: the channel itself carries a travelling band of light, brighter for more. Beautiful on glass.Brightness is a weak measure. It takes the glow that the energy layer needs, and the two cannot show together.Light, plus bloom.

My pick: beads as the quiet strength, trails with bloom as the bold one, and the swarm kept for a close-up of one selected channel. Pulses are the fallback when the whole cell is in view and for visitors who ask for less motion.

2How time is treated

One kind of channel passes millions of ions a second. No picture shows that one by one at true speed. There are two honest knobs: what one bead stands for, and how much time is slowed. Beads a second on the screen are the ions a second, divided by the ions in a bead and by the slow-down.

OptionWhat you seeWhat the legend must sayRisk
A token My pickTrue speed. Each bead stands for many ions."1 bead = 2 million ions."None, if the number is always on the picture.
Slow motion you set With itDrag the slider: time stretches and a bead stands for fewer ions. Near a million times slower, one bead is one ion."1 second here is 1 microsecond in the cell", beside the token.At strong slow-down the cell's own state hardly changes, so a slow change in the run looks frozen. That is true, and worth saying.
A loopA short stretch of the run, played again and again, with a bar that shows where in the loop you are.The loop's length in the cell's time, and the time it takes to play.The seam: the end must meet the start, or everything jumps. See choice 4.
On demandNothing moves until you ask. Then a part shows its flux for a few seconds.The same token, on the pulse.A pulse can be taken for an event in the cell. It is only the viewer showing a steady rate for a moment.
The rate sets how many My pickMore beads for more flux, all at one speed."Speed stands for nothing."Dense streams when rates differ a thousandfold: the token then needs steps.
The rate sets how fastEvenly spaced beads that run faster for more flux."Faster means more ions a second."Speeds are hard to compare, and a fast bead looks like a fast ion. The engine has no speed of an ion.

My pick: a token at true speed when flux mode opens, a slow-down slider beside it, and the rate setting how many beads pass. A loop is the natural clock once the engine's looping runs exist.

4Where the numbers come from

The viewer's rule is that something moves only where a rate stands behind it, and that a layer fed by a run is drawn where the run is shown. The Cell view of a species is not a run today. Three honest ways to put flux there, in the order I would take them.

The cell's own looping run First

You have asked the engine for looping replay packages of each species' cell, as the lead reports, with a first loop of the planarian's cell before the engine's rework is done. The Cell view then shows that run: engine numbers, for that cell.

Planarian cell · the engine's looping run (its name) · a loop of 2.0 s of the cell's time · slowed 3 times · 1 bead = 2 million ions

Needs: the loop, with the records below. Nothing in the contract forbids it once its rule 7 says so.

Typical values, as illustration

For a species or a part with no loop yet: numbers from a cited source, not from a run. This is the allowance you gave earlier, that the viewer may draw illustrative machinery driven by aggregate values if it says so.

Illustration: not a run · typical values for an animal cell (source) · 1 bead = 2 million ions

Needs: a source per number, and a new rule in the contract. Never on when the page opens.

Direction only

Which way each ion goes, and more or less of it in three steps, with no number at all. Sodium in, potassium out, the pump three for two.

Illustration: direction only · the number of beads stands for no rate

Needs: the same new rule. The weakest of the three, and the easiest to mistake for data.

The change the contract would need

Rule 7, replaced. Drawn where the run is shown; in the Cell view only from that cell's own run. A layer fed by a run is drawn in that run's replay: on the sheet, or on a close-up of one chosen cell's membrane, named with the run, the cell and the time. In the Cell view of a species it is drawn only from that species' own looping run, a run the engine made of that one cell and packaged to loop. The picture then names the run, says that it loops, and gives the loop's length in the cell's time and how much it is slowed. A species with no such run shows no run-fed layer in its Cell view.

Rule 3, one sentence more. A layer on the motion channel states its time on the picture: true speed, how much it is slowed, or the loop it plays; and what one token stands for.

A new rule, only if you allow illustration. Illustration in the Cell view is a class of its own, off when the page opens. Where a species has no looping run, flux may be shown in its Cell view only as illustration, in one of two declared forms: typical values, from a cited source and not from a run; or direction only, where the count of tokens stands for no number. It carries "Illustration: not a run" on the picture and in the legend for as long as it is shown, never shares a picture with a run-fed layer, and is not offered for a part the species' file says it lacks.

A new rule for decoration. Motion that is not a layer has no net direction, stops while any layer on the motion channel shows, and stops for a visitor who asks for reduced motion.

5The resting look

The approved look stays: glass, natural colours, labels on leader lines. Ten effects could deepen it. Switch each one on and off, alone or together, and watch the frame rate on your device. "As today" is close to what the Cell view does now.

EffectWhat it addsCost on the graphics cardHow it could misleadOn a weaker device
Studio reflections FirstGlass and wet surfaces catch soft lights instead of a pale dome. The largest gain for the least.None per frame: the surroundings are prepared once.A bright reflection can look like a mark. Keep the lights soft and large.Unchanged.
Filmic light FirstHighlights roll off instead of clipping; colours gain depth.Close to none.It shifts colours a little. Data marks and status colours must be left out of it.Unchanged.
Soft translucency FirstOrganelles glow faintly at their thin edges, as soft matter does. Less plastic.A few lines in the surface shader.Little.Unchanged.
Contact shade FirstSoft darkening where parts meet and under them. The inside of the cell gains depth.One or two extra passes over the screen at half size. Glass cannot take part: only solid parts.Darkness could be read as density. Keep it gentle.Half size, or off.
Surface textureThe folds of a mitochondrion, grain on the sheets of the ER, pores on the nucleus.A noise function in the shader, or small generated textures.Detail that no data stands behind. It stays scenery, as the page already says of such parts.Unchanged.
Glass with depthThe membrane bends what lies behind it and tints with its thickness.The scene is drawn a second time for the glass. Glass does not see other glass, so nested shells need care.Bending moves things from where they are, which hurts reading a labelled picture. At the scale of molecules light does not do this at all.Fall back to today's glass.
Depth hazeFar parts fade toward the page's colour, so near parts stand out.None.Faded could be read as planned or absent. Keep status in the dot beside the name.Unchanged.
Crowd drift FirstThe cytosol's crowd jostles slowly at random. The cell looks alive.None to speak of: the points move in the shader.Motion could be read as flux. It has no net direction, and it stops while flux shows.Unchanged.
Membrane shimmerThe membrane reads as a fluid sheet, not a hard shell.A few lines in the shader.A travelling ripple would look like a voltage wave. No waves: only a still, slow flicker.Unchanged.
BloomBright edges and highlights glow.Several blur passes at reduced size.Glow is a channel for data: the energy layer and the flux styles use it. At rest I would leave it off, so that switching flux on makes the cell light up.Off.

My pick: studio reflections, filmic light, soft translucency, contact shade and a drifting crowd first. They keep the look, add depth and life, and cost little. Bloom is kept for flux. Glass that bends light is the one I would leave out.

What is asked of you

  1. Which style? I would build beads, with trails as the bold strength, and keep the swarm for one channel in close-up.
  2. How is time shown when flux opens? I would open at true speed with a token, and put the slow-down slider beside it.
  3. How does flux come in? I would show a part's flux when it is selected, and add a Flux switch for all of it.
  4. May the Cell view show illustration where a species has no looping run yet, marked as such? If not, flux waits for each species' loop.
  5. Which resting effects? I would start with five: studio reflections, filmic light, soft translucency, contact shade, crowd drift.
  6. May anything move at rest? The drifting crowd and the shimmer are decoration. They stop for reduced motion and while flux shows. Say if you would rather have a still cell.

What the engine would be asked for

  1. A loop that closes: its last record equals its first, with how closely, and the amounts moved in each interval, so nothing is differenced across the seam.
  2. Enough intervals in a loop to follow how the rates change.
  3. For each kind of channel, how many channels it stands for and how open they are, so a stream can be spread over the forms drawn.
  4. Water, secreted signals and fuel as amounts moved, if they are to flow too: the note to the lead asks only for ions and energy.
  5. The run's own name for the label: which model, which revision, which cell.

These add to the nine requests in the note to the lead. They are requests, and the lead decides.
