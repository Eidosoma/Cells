Origin of life in the viewer: a GARD assembly

GARD, short for Graded Autocatalysis Replication Domain, is a model of how a droplet of fat-like molecules could carry heredity before there were genes. The droplet takes in molecules from its surroundings, grows, splits in two, and its daughters may keep its mix of molecules. The Plastic Heredity preprint was built on it. This page offers ways the viewer could show a GARD assembly, from one droplet to its lineages. Nothing here is built, and nothing of GARD is in Cells' engine yet. The engine's design note for GARD is proposed, on a development branch, not on main; this page uses its names and its records. Every sketch is live and is drawn from a small toy that this page runs when it opens. The toy follows GARD's rules as the preprint and the original papers print them, with a random seed and choices of its own. It is not Cells' engine, not the preprint's code and not its runs, and nothing it shows is a finding.

The choice is what to build, and in which order. I would add a heading, "Origin of life", to the species switch, with GARD as a model system rather than an organism, and give it a view of its own. I would build that view in this order: the composition strip and the similarity at each fission, under one time control; then the assembly drawn as a micelle; then the lineage tree; then the carpet of similarities and the map of compositions. The questions for you are near the end.

What GARD is, in plain words

Each card says whose statement it is: the preprint's, or the original model's as the GARD papers state it. The two differ in places, and the sketches keep them apart.

The molecules both

A hundred types, t000 to t099 in the engine's proposed names. They stand for amphiphiles, molecules with a water-loving head and an oily tail, as the lipids of a membrane have. The model gives a type no chemistry: it is a number, and what it does comes only from the table of catalysis below.

100 types: the preprint's Appendix F, and Gross and others 2014, Markovitch and Krasnogor 2018. Amphiphiles: the 2018 review.

An assembly is a list of counts both

How many molecules of each type it holds, and nothing else. The papers call the list its composition; the 2018 review calls it a compositional genome. The preprint starts each lineage at 40 molecules of 40 different types.

The preprint, Appendix F; the 2018 review, "Compositional homeostasis" and "Compositional information".

Mutual catalysis: a fixed table both

A table β (beta) of 100 × 100 numbers says how much a molecule of type j inside the assembly speeds the joining and the leaving of type i. Its numbers are drawn at random from a log-normal distribution, so most are tiny and a few are very large. A molecule therefore joins faster the more the molecules already inside favour it.

βij = exp(−4 + 4Zij), Z standard normal, diagonal kept

bi = 1 + (βx)i/N · join ai+ = kf ρi N bi · leave ai− = kb xi bi

with kf = 10−2, kb = 10−4, and every type held at ρi = 0.01 in a reservoir that never runs down. xi is the count of type i and N the assembly's size.

The preprint, Appendix F, for all six of its versions. The same form, written as one rate equation, is in Gross and others 2014 and in Markovitch and Krasnogor 2018 (which give ln β as normal with mean −4 and standard deviation 4).

Growth and splitting both

The assembly grows until it holds 80 molecules, then splits in two. In the preprint, one daughter carries the lineage on and the other is not followed. Its six versions differ in how the split is drawn (each molecule to either side with even odds, or exactly half), in which daughter carries on, and in how an overshoot past 80 is trimmed. The original papers run the same "single-lineage mode", splitting at a size Nmax into two of Nmax/2, and also run populations held at one size: Gross and others 2014 seed a thousand assemblies and keep their number fixed by a Moran process; the engine's note words that step as a fission that removes a member at random.

The preprint, Appendix F; Gross and others 2014, Methods; Markovitch and Krasnogor 2018 (Nmax = 100 there, 80 in the preprint).

Similarity H both

The cosine of the angle between two lists of counts: 1 when the two mixes are in the same proportions, whatever their sizes, and near 0 when they share little. A daughter half the size of its parent can still score 1.

H(x, y) = x · y / (‖x‖ ‖y‖)

The preprint, "Compositional similarity and boundary inheritance"; the 2018 review calls it the normalised dot product.

Inheritance, the threshold, and F12 the preprint's

At each fission the preprint compares the parent just before it splits with the daughter that carries on. H above 0.9 counts as inheritance; 0.9 or below is a break, so 0.9 itself is a break. The growth in between is not tested, and successive daughters are not compared. Its first endpoint, F12, asks of the next twelve fissions: was there a break, and, strictly after it, three inherited fissions in a row? It does not ask for a return to the old mix. A stricter event asks for eight inherited fissions whose daughters all resemble one another above 0.9 and all differ from the old parent (H at most 0.85).

The preprint: "In brief", the reader's guide, the section "F12 break-and-renewal endpoint", and Figure 4's caption. The engine's record states the same definition (docs/SCIENTIFIC_BASIS.md, main).

Composomes and compotypes the original model's

Some mixes reproduce themselves: as the assembly grows its proportions hold, so its daughters resemble it. The papers call such a self-preserving mix a composome: a run of assemblies, each closely like the one before it and the one after it. A group of similar composomes is a compotype, found by clustering (k-means). A lineage can sit in a composome for many generations, drift, and enter another; different tables β give one to seven. The preprint uses no composomes in its endpoints and did no clustering; it names mapping its episodes onto compotypes as a next test.

Gross and others 2014, Methods (the definition quoted); the 2018 review, "Replicating composomes"; Markovitch and Krasnogor 2018 (one to six compotypes in 10,000 runs); the preprint, "Plastic heredity and the composome debate".

In the engine's design note proposed

Proposed, on a development branch, not on main (docs/GARD_DESIGN.md, branch gard-design, e75b4b1f). Its names, which this page follows: an assembly holds integer counts of molecule types t000 to t099; it exchanges molecules with a held reservoir; the matrix β and the reservoir are the environment, never the assembly's state; one daughter continues the lineage; a population has a fixed number of slots.

Its laws: cells.reservoir_exchange@1, and the catalysis factor in two forms, cells.member_catalysis_at_state@1 (as published) and cells.member_catalysis@1 ("pair-consistent"). Its operators: cells.size_threshold_fission@1 and cells.daughter_continuation@1. It recommends abstract assemblies first, run by exact single-molecule events, with 100 types in a dense log-normal β. H is worked out in the engine at each fission; F12, composomes and compotypes in validation/gard/.

An open finding, under review: the note reports that the published single-event kernel is not microscopically reversible, and proposes the pair-consistent form beside it.

What GARD does not have both

  • No shape, no size in space, no membrane, no place and no neighbours. The 2018 review treats the assembly as well stirred, with no structure in space.
  • No charge, voltage or energy, and no chemistry of its molecules beyond the table β.
  • The reservoir and the table are fixed. The assembly neither writes nor inherits them (the preprint).
  • No clock of real life. Its time is that of its rate constants, and no measured chemistry stands behind them.
  • Whether such assemblies can evolve is disputed. Vasas and others (2010) argued that they cannot evolve open-endedly; the GARD authors replied (2012, and the 2018 review). The preprint sets the exchange out.

So any shape, place or motion in a picture of GARD is the picture's choice, and the picture says so.

The toy the sketches run on

No GARD run of Cells' engine exists, so the sketches below share one small toy that this page runs once when it opens, in well under a second, and then replays under one clock.

A toy in this page: not Cells' engine, not the preprint's code and not its runs. Nothing it shows is a finding about GARD.

Taken from the sources: 100 types; the table drawn as βij = exp(−4 + 4Zij); the boost, join and leave rates and their constants; the constant reservoir; a start at 40 molecules of 40 types; growth to 80 (all from the preprint's Appendix F); one molecule joining or leaving at a time, chosen in proportion to every rate, an exact half drawn at random at the fission, and the first daughter carrying on (the preprint's version "T2-02"); H and the strict threshold of 0.9 (the preprint); a population kept at one size by a Moran process (Gross and others 2014), here a random other assembly removed when one splits, as the engine's note words it.

The page's own: the random seed (16, picked because its picture shows long inherited stretches and breaks); the one table β that seed draws; 120 fissions; the waiting times of a stochastic simulation, used only to order the population's events; a population of 24 assemblies, which takes three of its fissions for each fission of the lineage so that both reach their end together.

Its kernel is the published form: the catalytic factor bi is taken at the current state for a join and a leave alike, as the preprint states it (in the engine's proposed note, cells.member_catalysis_at_state@1). The note has an open finding on this form, under review, and proposes a pair-consistent form beside it. The toy offers only the published one.

The toy has not run: the page's script did not start. The descriptions below still hold.

A. The entry: a heading “Origin of life” in the species switch

The species switch lists its entries under headings that the files give (Animals, Bacteria, Plants). GARD would stand under a new heading, as a model system and not an organism, with four levels of its own in place of molecules, cell, tissue, organ and organism.

You see and do
The species list gains the heading "Origin of life" and one entry, "GARD assembly", with a small line saying it is a model and not an organism. Choosing it puts the four levels on the scale rail: Molecules, One assembly, Population, Lineages. The card says what Cells models of it in the record's own words: nothing today. Nothing of the Cell view (membranes, organelles, the Protein atlas's doors) appears, because GARD has none of them.
Told apart from a species
Its own heading; its own levels; no taxon and no organism name; a card that begins "a model of how heredity could begin"; and, in the switch, the small line "a model, not an organism". The Protein atlas has nothing for it and says so.
Where it fits
Three ways. (i) A species file with a new group: the heading would cost nothing (the switch reads its headings from the files), but the species contract requires a cell level and an organism, and its levels come from a fixed list (tools/lib/species.py, src/lenses/cell.js); GARD would be drawn as a cell, which it is not. (ii) A new kind of entry beside species (my pick): a file under its own contract that shares the species file's identity, sources, licence classes and "what Cells models" blocks, and has its own fixed list of levels (molecules, assembly, population, lineage); the switch learns a second kind of entry, and choosing it opens a view of its own. (iii) Experiments only: GARD runs listed in the Experiments view, with nothing in the switch; cheapest, but it hides what the user asked to see. A cell type does not fit: a cell type belongs to a species and is a cell.

B. One assembly, growing and splittingMy pick: the micelle

One bead for each molecule, so the count is exact; everything else is the picture's choice, since GARD gives an assembly no shape. Three ways to draw it: a micelle, as the GARD papers' own figures sketch one; a loose cluster that claims no structure; and a vesicle, shown here to say why I would not use it.

You see and do
The assembly, bead by bead. A molecule that joins flies in from the reservoir and takes its place; one that leaves flies out. At 80 the assembly pulls apart into two of 40, split as the run says; the one that carries on stays in the middle, and the other drifts off, labelled "not followed". Drag to turn it. The three types that lead in the run wear a colour and a shape; the other 97 are grey. Choosing a bead names its type.
The three shapes
Micelle (my pick): heads out, tails in, on a ball whose surface grows with the count. The GARD papers sketch their assembly so (the 2018 review's first figure draws a small micelle), and the size fits: the review says that with a hundred types, composomes appear only in assemblies the size of micelles. Cluster: beads loosely packed, no heads or tails; it claims the least, and it is the stand-in on a phone and with less motion. Vesicle: a closed double layer. Eighty molecules cannot make one: the 2018 review puts a small vesicle 0.2 µm across at about a million molecules, and says GARD at such counts has not been explored. So the vesicle looks sparse here, and the picture would mislead.
From the engine
In the engine's note (proposed, on a development branch, not on main): the counts, assembly_count, an amount on [row, species, assembly], saved by default at each fission's two rows only: the parent just before it and the daughter just after (time.event_rows). So by default a run gives each daughter and each grown parent, and not the molecules in between. Drawing each molecule as it arrives needs the note's event trace (time, type, ±1; about 62 entries per fission), saved only on request, or rows at a declared cadence: a question for the lead. Without either, the picture steps from the daughter to the grown parent and says that the growth between was not saved. The split comes from the fission table: fission_split (the first daughter's counts; the other is the parent less these) and fission_followed. No geometry: the note recommends abstract assemblies with no volume, area or charge, so on my reading the view's space would be null (the note does not say so in these words). If the engine later binds the same laws to vesicles in the physical world (the note's option B), the shape becomes the run's, and the picture follows it.
One and thousands
One assembly is at most 80 beads (100 in the original papers). A population of thousands is drawn as small balls, one per assembly, sized by its count and marked by its leading type; the beads appear only for the one chosen.
Cost
Medium, about 2 to 3 agent-days: bead layout and relaxation, the fission, the flights, picking, and the three shapes.
Phone, keys, less motion, no 3D
On a phone the cluster is drawn by default and no tails. The arrow keys step one fission back or forth. With less motion asked for, nothing flies and nothing turns by itself: beads appear and vanish in place, and a fission is shown in two states. Without 3D, a drawn still of the same beads stands in, and the list at the foot of the page gives each fission's counts.

C. Its composition: the strip, the wheel and the tape

The list of counts drawn as shares: the 2018 review's "compositional genome". The types keep their places (t000 to t099, in order) so that a change of mix reads as a change of widths, not as a reshuffle.

You see and do
Strip: one bar, as long as the assembly is large (full at 80), cut into the types it holds, in order; at the fission it parts into two bars of half the length, one carrying on and one not followed. Wheel: the same shares round a ring, with the count in the middle; it reads best at a glance and on a phone. Tape: each fission's grown composition as a column, types from t000 at the top to t099 at the bottom, stronger in colour for more molecules, in the form Markovitch and Krasnogor (2018) use for their "GARD tapes"; long runs of the same mix show as level bands. Choose a part to name its type and count.
From the engine
In the engine's note (proposed, on a development branch, not on main): the strip and the wheel read assembly_count at the row shown; the tape reads it at each fission's parent row. Shares are counts over the size, a division the viewer states. The list's joins and leaves for each fission are differences of the run's joined_total and left_total (cumulative since the lineage began) between two fission rows.
One and thousands
One strip per assembly. For a population, strips stack into a tape of assemblies at one time, sorted by similarity to a chosen one, or the population's mean strip with its spread.
Cost
Small, about 1 to 1.5 agent-days; plain 2D drawing.
Phone, keys, less motion, no 3D
2D, so it needs no 3D. On a phone the wheel is the default. With less motion, widths jump rather than ease. As a list: the types and counts at each fission, in the table at the foot of the page.

D. Similarity: at each fission, and the carpet over time

Two different comparisons, kept apart. On the left, the preprint's: the parent just before it splits against the daughter that carries on, where its threshold of 0.9 belongs. On the right, the original papers' carpet: every grown composition against every other, at the same point of each cycle, with no threshold of its own.

You see and do
Left: H at each fission, a dot for each, with the 0.9 line; inherited fissions are filled dots and breaks are rings, so the two differ by shape as well as by place. Switch to the parent against the daughter that is not followed (the run's record holds both), or to the assembly's own birth against itself grown (the growth F12 does not test); for those the 0.9 line is not drawn, since F12 applies it to the followed daughter alone. Right: the carpet, fission against fission, as the 2018 review's figure 3d draws it over 2,000 cycles: strong squares along the diagonal are stretches where the mix held; a strong patch away from it is a mix that came back. Ringing the pairs above 0.9 is the picture's choice, offered to read the squares, and is off at first. Choose a dot or a cell to read its value.
From the engine
Left: the run's own boundary_similarity (proposed, on a development branch, not on main): a level on the fission table, [fission, 2], the parent against each daughter, so it gives both "carried on" and "other daughter". E. Composomes: a map of compositions that a lineage visits and leaves

Each grown composition becomes a point in a map, and the lineage leaves a trail. Where it stays, points crowd; where it breaks away, the trail jumps. Which crowds are composomes the page does not say: that is for a run or its analysis to state, by a named method.

You see and do
A map turned in 3D: each point is one grown composition; the trail joins them in order, solid across an inherited fission and dashed across a break; the point of the moment is ringed, and the growing assembly moves between points as molecules join. Arrows show which way each leading type lies, so a crowd can be read as "mostly type 74". Switch to the population to see its 24 assemblies as a cloud. Choose a point to go to that fission.
How the map is made
The axes are the three directions along which the lineage's compositions differ most (principal components), worked out by this page from the counts: a layout, the picture's choice, like the hexagonal layout of a replayed sheet. The 2018 review draws its lineage so, in its figure 2b. The page runs no clustering and draws no composome: a crowd is where the lineage stayed, and nothing more.
From the engine
In the engine's note (proposed, on a development branch, not on main): assembly_count at each fission's parent row. Composomes and compotypes come from the analysis in validation/gard/ (the note's first experiment registers its clustering in advance). Only where that analysis saves an assignment, with its method and settings, does the map name and ring a composome.
One and thousands
It holds thousands best of all: a cloud of points, dense where assemblies crowd. A population of 1,000 over 50,000 fissions (the size Gross and others use) would be drawn as density, with one lineage's trail picked out.
Cost
Medium, about 1.5 to 2 agent-days: the projection, the trail, picking, the cloud.
Phone, keys, less motion, no 3D
On a phone the map is drawn flat, the first two axes only. With less motion it does not turn by itself. Without 3D, a flat drawing of the first two axes stands in. As a list it has no good form; the table's H column says where the lineage broke away.

F. Lineages: a tree across fissions

Every fission makes two branches. Each branch is marked by whether the daughter kept its parent's mix above the threshold: solid if it did, dashed if it broke.

You see and do
One lineage: the preprint's mode. The daughters that carry on form a spine across the fissions; each fission's other daughter is a short stub ending in a cross, "not followed". Breaks show as dashed spine. A population: the original papers' mode, held at one size. The tree branches at every fission; when one assembly splits, a random other is removed, so most branches end in a cross. The ancestry of the assemblies alive at the clock's time is bold, the rest faint. Choose a branch to read its H.
From the engine
The fission table (proposed, on a development branch, not on main): fission_time, fission_slot, fission_parent, fission_daughters, fission_followed, fission_split, fission_similarity; and its events: fission at the slot, retirement of the parent, birth of the daughter that carries on, while the other daughter is born and retired at the same time. The slot keeps its place and changes its occupant (occupant, generation), as cell replacement does today. In population mode, which the note leaves for later (cells.population_replacement@1, a Moran step), a fission writes its two daughters into the parent's slot and the removed member's slot: the 24 lanes drawn here are those slots. The note asks the run view's owner to take these events from the run's fission table, not from a schedule.
One and thousands
One lineage is a line. A population of thousands is drawn as the ancestry of the living alone (most branches end within a few fissions), with the rest folded into counts along it.
Cost
Medium, about 2 to 3 agent-days: the tree's layout, pruning, picking, and following a branch through time.
Phone, keys, less motion, no 3D
2D. On a phone the population tree shows the ancestry of the living only. With less motion it grows in steps. As a list: the fissions in order, each with its parent, its two daughters and its H.

G. Time: one control for all of it

Every sketch on this page follows the one clock at the foot of the screen, as the user asked of the Cell view: one control, the same as the replay's, slower and faster.

You see and do
Play and pause, a step back or forward of one fission, a slider across the fissions, and a speed. Every view of the run moves together: the beads, the strip, the dots, the carpet, the map and the tree.
What its speed counts
Fissions for each second of watching, from a quarter to eight, and "molecule by molecule" at the slow end, eight joins or leaves a second. GARD has no clock of real life (its rate constants stand on no measured chemistry), so "true speed" and "slowed a thousand times" are not offered: the control states the run's own clock, and the run's view says what that clock is.
From the engine

Cost
Small, about half a day to a day: the replay's timeline with a second ladder of speeds, read from the run.
Phone, keys, less motion

Recommendation

  1. The entry first (A), as a new kind of entry beside species, so that "Origin of life" stands in the switch with an honest card while the engine designs GARD. Its view says, at each level, what it will show and that nothing is in the engine yet.
  2. Then the core of a run, on the first engine run of GARD: the composition strip (C), the similarity at each fission (D, left) and the one clock (G). They are 2D, cheap, true to the preprint's own measure, and they work on a phone and as a list.

  4. Then the tree (F) for one lineage, and for a population once the engine has one.

What the viewer would work out itself, and say so: shares (a count over the size); the other daughter's counts as the parent less fission_split, as the note defines them; joins and leaves per fission as differences of the totals; H between any two saved compositions, as a fifth declared derivation (a change to the layer contract, with a check against the run's own H at every fission); and the map's layout. Never the viewer's: the rates, which molecule joins next, the inheritance verdict, F12, and composome clustering.

Where the run view does not fit yet, by the note's own list for the view's owner: fission events must come from the run's fission table, not from a schedule; the fission table should be named as an event record, since 100 fissions over 10,000 lanes are a million events; the time unit is nominal, not seconds; and the writer's first version accepts no family but the existing composite.

Sizes. A lineage of 100 fissions at two rows each is 200 × 100 counts, about 40 kB as 16-bit integers, with the fission table beside it; the event trace adds about 6,200 entries. A population of 1,000 over 50,000 fissions would be saved in parts or thinned, and the run would say which.

Questions for you

  1. Should GARD stand in the species switch under a new heading "Origin of life", as a model system and not a species? I recommend yes, as a new kind of entry with its own four levels.
  2. Which shape should the assembly have: micelle, cluster or vesicle? I recommend the micelle, labelled as the picture's choice, with the cluster on a phone and with less motion.
  3. Should a GARD run play in the entry's own view, or in the Experiments view? I recommend the entry's own view, with the Experiments view listing GARD runs and opening them there.
  4. Should the time control count fissions rather than seconds? I recommend fissions per second of watching, with molecule by molecule at the slow end and no "true speed"; the engine's note also takes the fission count as GARD's clock.
  5. Should the 0.9 line be drawn only on the parent-to-followed-daughter comparison, as the analysis defines it? I recommend yes; elsewhere a threshold is the picture's choice and is said to be.
  6. Should composomes be named only where a run or its analysis names them? I recommend yes: the map shows where a lineage stays and names nothing itself.
  7. Should this page's toy ever enter the built viewer? I recommend no: the viewer draws the engine's runs, and its tests use a marked fixture of invented numbers.
  8. Should one lineage come first and populations later? I recommend yes: it is the preprint's mode and the engine's first, and a population comes later in the engine too.
  9. When the engine runs both forms of its kernel side by side, should the viewer show a matching pair of runs under one clock? I recommend yes, each labelled with the law it ran, and nothing said of which is right.
