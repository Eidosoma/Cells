Showing what cannot be seen

Ten ways to draw voltages, charge, fields, forces and flows in the 3D viewer. Each is tied to a quantity the engine has or plans, and each has a sketch. Nothing here is built.

The choice is which to build first. I would start with ideas 1 and 2, the voltage pattern across a sheet: they need only what today's runs saved. The rest of the page says what each of the others would take.

What the engine can feed today

The two examples in the request, voltage gradients and electrostatic forces, stand very differently.

Voltage gradients: yes

Today's saved runs hold one membrane voltage per cell at each record, with the junctions between cells. The gradient across a tissue is the difference between neighbours. Concentrations per cell are saved too.

Flows and energy: computed, not saved

The engine computes ion fluxes through channels, pumps and junctions, the potentials inside and outside each cell, and energy ledgers. The runs the viewer has did not save them.

Forces and fields in space: not as such

The engine has no force on a single molecule. The electrical push on ions lives inside its transport laws, and can be drawn from them. A field between cells needs runs whose surroundings are not held at 0 V.

Works on today's saved runs

Four ideas that need no change in the engine. The first two show a voltage pattern across a sheet. The other two answer the question about electrostatic forces at the scale of one cell.

Voltage relief

SheetBuild first
You see
Each cell stands as high as its membrane voltage. A depolarised patch rises as a plateau, and the border between two voltage states is a cliff you can walk around.
Drawn with
Height and a one-hue tint on the sheet's own glass cells, with lines at chosen voltages.
Needs
Membrane voltage per cell, in volts. Saved today.
Honest by
Height repeats the colour and adds nothing to it. A bar says how many millivolts one step is. Nothing is smoothed between cells.
Cost
Small to build, light to draw.

3

Push and pull on each ion

Cell
You see
For each ion, the push of its concentration difference, the pull of the voltage, and what is left over. It shows why potassium leaks out while sodium is held back from rushing in.
Drawn with
Glass arrows through the membrane, in millivolts, one group per ion.
Needs
Voltage and concentrations in the cell (saved), with the composition outside and the temperature (in each run's definition).
Honest by
The arrows are the two terms of the engine's own transport law. An arrow is a drive, not a flow, and the formula stands in the legend.
Cost
Small to build, light to draw.

4

Charge on the membrane

Cell
You see
A membrane voltage is a thin layer of unbalanced charge on the two faces of the membrane. Marks crowd the faces at rest and thin out as the cell depolarises. The bulk stays neutral.
Drawn with
Plus and minus marks stippled on the glass membrane, as dense as the charge per area.
Needs
Voltage (saved), with the membrane's capacitance and area (in the definition): charge is capacitance times voltage.
Honest by
The count is real: one mark stands for a stated number of charges. Where each mark sits is illustration, and the page says so.
Cost
Small to medium to build, light to draw.

Ion traffic

Cell
You see
Ions crossing through each kind of channel and pump at the rate the engine computes: sodium streaming in, potassium out, the pump carrying three out for two in.
Drawn with
Beads moving through the glass channel forms, one hue per ion with its name beside it.
Needs
Flux per channel population and per pump, in femtomoles per second. Computed, not saved.
Honest by
Channels are modelled as populations, so a bead is a token of a rate: one bead stands for a stated number of ions per second. No bead moves without a flux behind it.
Cost
Medium to build, moderate to draw.

How it would be built

Each idea is a quantity, a mark and a legend. Kept apart, a new quantity becomes a few lines of data, and a new kind of mark becomes one small module.

A. Layers and marks (recommended)B. One view per quantityC. Fields as textures
What it isA quantity layer is data. It names a record of the run and one mark from a small set of mark modules.Each quantity gets a view of its own, hand-made.Every quantity is resampled onto a grid, and general graphics passes draw it: flowing textures, volumes, particles.
Adding oneA new quantity with a known mark: data only. A new mark: one module.New code for every quantity.Data only, once the passes exist.
CombiningEach mark says which channel it uses: colour, height, links, motion, glow. One layer per channel, so voltage relief and ion beads can show together and two colourings cannot.Not possible without a third view.Free, and easy to overload.
HonestyEach mark sits on the thing the engine computed it for: a cell, a junction, a channel. The layer carries the unit and how the layer is represented.Depends on each view.A grid invents detail between cells that the engine does not have.
CostThe first layer costs most, since it brings the contract and the switch. Later ones are small.Even, and it never falls.Highest. Worth it only when the engine has a real field on a grid.

My recommendation is A. Its mark modules can later include one from C, for the day the engine has a potential on a real grid. A layer would be declared like this:

{
  "id": "voltage-relief",
  "label": "Membrane voltage",
  "quantity": { "record": "vm", "lives_on": "cell", "unit": "V", "kind": "level" },
  "mark": "relief",
  "channels": ["height", "colour"],
  "scale": { "from": -0.080, "to": -0.020, "ramp": "one hue", "show_as": "mV" },
  "representation": "as the run view states it for the record's layer"
}

What the engine would be asked for

  1. Save what it already computes, when a run asks for it: fluxes through channels, pumps and junctions, junction voltages, the potentials inside and outside each cell, and the energy ledgers.
  2. A direction for every signed record: which way is positive, from which thing to which. The viewer then draws a flow's direction without reading a driver's habits.
  3. Positions for the outside nodes and faces, where a world has a space between cells.
  4. A short window at fine time steps around a fast event, beside the usual sparse records.
  5. The owner of each record: which law or operator computed it. With the modular rework and its levels of detail, a layer can then say what stands behind a number and how exact it is. Both are in development, not on main.

What is asked of you

  1. Which ideas first? I would build 1 and 2 together on the replayed sheet, then 3 and 4 in the cell view, and ask the engine for what 5 and 6 need.
  2. How bold? Relief, glow and moving beads can be strong or quiet. The sketches lean quiet.
  3. May motion stand for a rate? My rule would be: something moves only where the engine gives a flux.
  4. Only on real runs, or also in the cell view with example numbers, marked as illustration?
  5. Should the five requests go to the lead now, or after the first two layers show what they are worth?

What others do, in brief

The note beside this page says what each technique is good and bad at. These are the pages it rests on.

  • Colour that tells the truth and survives colour-blindness: Crameri, Shephard and Heron 2020.
  • Potential on a molecule's surface, red to blue around white: ChimeraX, the coulombic command; shells of equal potential: APBS with PyMOL.
  • Arrival times of an electrical wave in heart tissue: openCARP, electrical mapping.
  • A real voltage pattern seen with a dye, the frog embryo's "electric face": Tufts University, 2011.
  • Flow as moving particles on the graphics card: Cesium, GPU-powered wind; evenly spaced field lines: Kitware.
  • Stress in a living sheet of cells: Tambe and others 2011.
  • Where illustration may take licence, and must say so: Goodsell, Olson and Forli 2020.
