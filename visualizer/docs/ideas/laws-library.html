The Laws shelf, opened into a library

Five ways the Engine view's Laws shelf could open into a 3D library of the engine's declared pieces: its channel laws, junction laws, pumps, operators, kinds and solvers, and the models they plug into. Nothing here is built. Every sketch is live and is drawn in this page from the list of pieces read from the engine's code on 16 November 2026. The list keeps apart what is on main, what is on the landing candidate (not on main yet), and what only a design note names.

The choice is which to build. I would build the racks: model frames in front, with a slot for each piece a model declares, and the whole library on a wall behind them. It shows how pieces plug into a model, and it still holds a thousand pieces. The rest of the page says what each option needs.

These and the landing candidate are the project's working branches; they are not in the public repository until they land read how with the viewer's own static reader (tools/lib/static_eval.py) and git show; no engine code was imported or run

Today's Laws shelf, and where it runs out

The Engine view draws the engine as a stack of shelves. The Laws shelf holds 26 boxes. A box is a group of files, not a law.

  • One box holds many laws. The channel catalogue's box stands for 56 laws, and its panel lists them as text under the 34 units they act on, seven units at a time.
  • Boxes are groups of files. The laws in one file share a box. Laws from the rework, which are declared one by one, have no place of their own: on the shelf they are one designed box, "Laws as declared pieces".
  • Nothing shows how a piece plugs into a model. The Models shelf and the Laws shelf are joined only by lines between boxes.

Where a piece's status is kept

  • Nowhere per piece. The plan's levels (implemented, guarded, numerically qualified, judged) are kept per item in the status table of docs/CURRENT_PLAN.md, with the evidence in PROGRESS.md and the choices in docs/DECISIONS.md.
  • provenance: "parity" on a declaration is a source tag ("moving is not calibrating"), not a level.
  • A composition is not qualified by its parts: "integration grants no acceptance; judged status only from a registered protocol" (core/pump_composite_spec.py).
  • The sketches show no status. An opened piece gives its operator's status where the Engine view records one, with the source, and otherwise says "no level recorded for this piece".

What every option shares

The options differ in the room. These parts are the same in each, so they are built once.

The way in
Choosing the Laws shelf in the Engine view opens the library, and a back step returns to the stack. A part elsewhere in the viewer that names a law, such as a protein unit, opens the library on that piece. The address names it (for example lens=engine&law=kv1p5_mh@1).
Find and filter
One search field (name, unit, file) and filters for family, where the piece is (main, landing candidate, design note), model, and namespace once modules arrive. A status filter is offered only for levels the record states.
The opened piece
One card, the same in every option: what it computes, in its own module's words; what it declares; which models and compositions it plugs into; what it saves for replays; its status with the evidence and sources, or that none is recorded; its files at the revision read.
The list
Every piece as a plain table (at the foot of this page). It is the way in by keyboard, the stand-in where 3D cannot be drawn, and what a screen reader reads.
The rule for marks
Shape tells where a piece is: solid on main, banded on the landing candidate (not on main yet), an outline when only a design note names it (the card says whether that note is on main or on a development branch), faint and dotted for an empty slot. Blue marks only what you chose or found. No colour stands for a status.

The racks: a model is a frame, a piece is a card in its slotMy pick

The model frames stand in front: the one inner model, the composite over it, and the three earlier definitions that are now compositions. Behind them the library wall holds every piece in its family's column, and the older families stand below as closed frames. Choose a bar on the wall and a line runs to every slot it fills. Choose a frame's name and its pieces light on the wall.

You see and do
Frames with a labelled slot row for each kind of piece a model declares. For resolved_pump_tissue@3: channel laws, the junction law, pumps, routes, fuel, the heat booking, kinds and solvers. The composite holds @3 and an ordered column of operators. You browse the wall, filter it, find a piece, or start from a frame.
An opened piece
The shared card, whose "plugs into" line names the frames the lines reach.
The modular structure
This is the structure itself. A model is a composition of declared pieces, and a world compiles only what it declares (D172). A combination is a definition and not a family (D143). The dashed slot after the fifth channel law is the next free registry code. The sketch's @3 frame holds what @3's registries hold on the landing candidate; a world's frame would hold what its definition declares.
As pieces are added
The wall comes from the manifest's list, and a frame's slots from that model's declared structure. A contributed module's pieces join the wall under their namespace (D185). Nobody redraws anything.
At three sizes
Today the tallest column holds 39 bars (the other process laws). At about 300 it holds 67, and at a thousand 222, so that each bar is a line in a barcode: you find a piece by search, and the lines to its frames do the rest. Frames do not grow with the library.
Phone, keys, less motion, no 3D
On a phone the sketch draws the wall above the frames, small. A built version would open a chosen frame to fill the picture. The arrow keys step through the pieces in view, and shift with an arrow turns the picture. With less motion asked for, nothing eases and the lines simply appear. Without 3D, each frame is a list of its slots, and the full list below stands in for the wall.
Cost and data
Medium to large. Today: @3's channel slots from LAW_CODES on main, and its other slots from the registries in models/inner_laws.py and the built-in kinds, which are on the landing candidate only. The composite's operator list comes from the registry. A world's own frame would show the subset its definition declares (InnerStructure). Every other frame's slots, and "used by" for each piece, need the manifest.

The stacks: a reading room of bookcases

A round room entered from the Engine view, with one bookcase for each group of families: channel laws, membrane transport, the inner model's parts, operators, solvers and steps, and the other process laws. A shelf is a family, and a piece is a spine with its name. Turn to browse. A chosen spine slides out.

You see and do
Cases in an arc around you, a plate on each shelf, and a spine for every piece. Find lights the spines that match and dims the rest. The family list turns the room to that shelf.
An opened piece
The shared card.
The modular structure
Only by family. The room says what kinds of piece exist and how many, not which model uses them.
As pieces are added
A spine's shelf comes from its declared kind and family. A new family is a new shelf, and a full case starts the next case round the room.
At three sizes
Today six cases stand in front of you, with room for 1,080 spines. At about 300 the same six cases fill up. At a thousand there are ten, two thirds of the way round the room, and you find a piece by search rather than by eye.
Phone, keys, less motion, no 3D

The plugboard: pieces wired to the state they share

Six rails run across a board: the plan's shared-state entities, from the compartment to the extracellular environment. Every piece is a module above them, wired to the rails it changes or provides (a solid end) and the rails it reads (an open end). Choose a module to light its wires, or a rail to light everything that touches it.

You see and do
Modules in family blocks above the rails, with wires down to them. Pieces with no declaration read have no wires and say so.
An opened piece
The shared card, with its wires listed.
The modular structure
The contract seen from the state: mechanisms that affect the same thing change the same state (the plan's central abstraction), and each field has one provider. It shows ownership and conflicts, which is what the composite checks.
Where the wires come from
An operator's wires come from its contract (what it provides, and what it reads as declared), placed on a rail by the Engine view's table of fields. A law's wires are drawn from its kind and are dashed, because no law declares its reads and writes yet. Fields the table does not name are placed by their prefix: that is the page's reading.
At three sizes
Today it is readable. At a few hundred the wires weave. At a thousand the sketch packs the modules tighter and the wires merge. A built version would gather them into family blocks with bundled wires, which the sketch does not draw, and you would read single wires by search.
Phone, keys, less motion, no 3D
Weak on a phone: the wires are thin. The arrow keys step through modules, and up and down step through rails. Without 3D, the list gives each piece's rails in words.
Cost and data
Large. It depends wholly on the manifest's reads and writes; today only the 19 operators are truly wired.

No new room. The Laws shelf stays where it is in the Engine view, and each family becomes a drawer in it that slides out with its pieces in rows on its floor. Operators and solvers pull out of the shelves they already stand on.

You see and do
The stack's shelves with drawers. A click on a drawer front, the family list or a search opens the drawer that holds a match, and a click on a tile opens the piece.

A tile per piece and a drawer per family.

My recommendation: the racks

  • It answers both of the user's sentences. The wall gives room for a great many pieces. The frames show the modular structure: a model is a composition of declared pieces (D172), and a new combination is a definition, not a new family (D143).
  • It is drawn from what the engine declares. The slots of @3's frame are the pieces its registries hold on the landing candidate, and a world's frame would hold what its definition declares (InnerStructure). The composite's column is the registry of operators. Only the plain words are curated.
  • It holds at a thousand without redrawing. A new piece is a new bar on the wall. A frame grows only when a model declares more.
  • It keeps the record's honesty. A frame never takes a status from its parts. The empty "next free code" slot is the engine's own rule that a new channel law takes the next code and no code is reused.
  • With two parts from the other options. Its index is the card catalogue's search, with the list as the stand-in. Later, once the manifest gives every piece's reads and writes, the plugboard can be a second face of the same wall.

To build first: the shared card, the list and the search, with the wall and the frame of @3, from what can be read today. Then the other frames and the "used by" lines, when the declarations manifest lands.

The lead's answer of 16 November 2026 was that a static reader gets the ids and the module lists, and that the full declarations need an import. What is asked of you

  • Which option? I recommend the racks, with the catalogue's search as its index.
  • Should the older families' laws stand in the library before they move into the one model? I recommend yes, on their own shelves, marked as in an older family.
  • Should a piece show a status only where the record states one? I recommend yes, and "no level recorded" everywhere else.
  • Build now from what can be read, or wait for the manifest? I recommend building the wall, the card and the list now, marked as read from the code, and switching to the manifest when it lands.
  • A view of its own, or a room inside the Engine view? I recommend a room entered from the Laws shelf, with its own address and a way back.
  • How should it look? I recommend the Engine view's glass and slate, not wood and leather, so that the library reads as part of the same map.

What the engine would be asked for

  • The declarations manifest that D185 adds, with the fields the viewer needs. A draft feature request is in the note beside this page, ready for the lead.
  • Which ids are pieces: laws, kinds, operators and solvers, and not step methods, random streams or check domains.
  • A model's declared structure for every model and composition, as InnerStructure gives it for one definition of @3.
  • Who owns each record in the run view, so that a piece can say what it saves for a replay.

These are requests, and the lead decides. The note lists the questions that only the lead can answer.
