# The African clawed frog, Xenopus laevis, in the viewer

Status: **added on 19 November 2026** as a ninth species, with its species file, its side of the
Protein atlas and its own proteins on shelves. It was decided the same day that the frog is added
as a whole species, by eight rules for its proteins (below). The adult is drawn, and its life
stages stand on a strip in the Scope view (below): the fertilised egg is drawn, four stages between
are named and not drawn. Its bench of machines and the vault are not part of this file. Everything
here is generated by `tools/build_xenopus_atlas.py` from saved downloads, the same bytes every
time, but for the stages, which are kept by hand in `data/species/stages/xenopus.json` and checked
by the build against the open papers they cite.

This is the viewer's reference data. It says nothing about the engine: Cells models a generic
cell and a sheet of such cells, and the frog lends names and outlines.

## What Cells models of it

`modelled.state` is `generic`, in the mouse's words: "Cells models a generic cell and a sheet of
such cells, not this animal." The sheet stands for skin-like tissue and is drawn on a hind foot
for the scales only; the foot and the frog are placeholder outlines, and nothing at those scales
is simulated. The species file says nothing more of the engine.

## The cell, the sheet and the outline

The cell and the sheet are the mouse's and the planarian's: one flat-sided cell of a sheet that
stands for skin-like tissue, its shape and sizes the picture's choice. The organ is a webbed hind
foot, the picture's choice of a flat part a visitor knows, on which the sheet can lie level; its
length, breadth and shape are the picture's choice, since no measurement of a foot was read.

The body is drawn from one open paper, Böswald and others (2022), Animals 12(21):2986
(doi:10.3390/ani12212986, CC BY 4.0), Table 1: for 198 adult females kept as laboratory animals,
a snout-vent length of 12.1 ± 1.0 cm, a width directly behind the forelimbs of 5.1 ± 0.5 cm and
directly in front of the hindlimbs of 6.2 ± 0.7 cm, and (for 190) a thigh width of 3.4 ± 0.3 cm;
for 40 males a snout-vent length of 7.7 ± 0.3 cm. The female is drawn. Where along the body the
two widths stand (36 and 86 per cent of its length), the height, the head's shape, every limb's
length, the feet and the pose are the picture's choice. The build reads the paper's licence and
DOI in its full text, the sentences that say what each measure is, and the cells of its Table 1,
and stops if one is gone. The paper's record is Europe PMC's, from the gathering round's search.

Six stages, in the order of the standard table of normal development (the NF stages,
`schema/SPECIES_STAGES.md`), each named as Zahn and others (2022), Development 149(14):dev200356
(doi:10.1242/dev.200356, CC BY 4.0), name it in their running text:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fertilised egg | yes: one cell, a sphere 1.2 mm across | | "NF stages 1-6: zygote and cleavage", paragraph 1 |
| 2 | Two-cell embryo | no | about 90 min at 23 °C (the first cell division) | the same paragraph |
| 3 | Four-cell embryo | no | not written: the paper gives it from the first division (20 to 30 min later, at 23 °C) | the same paragraph |
| 35-36 | Free-swimming tadpole | no | | "NF stages 29-38: late tailbud and free-swimming tadpoles", paragraph 2 |

The paragraphs are counted in the saved full text. It was decided on 19 November 2026 by the
viewer's coordinator, within the user's choice of a strip with just two stages to go between, that
the egg and the adult are drawn and these four are named; that the frog opens on its adult; and
that no stage gives a word of its own for what Cells models of it, so the species' own word stands.
The same evening the owner decided that **the frog opens on its fertilised egg** (`opens_on` in the
stages block): choosing the frog shows the egg, and its adult, the form the species file
describes, is a press away on the strip; an address that names no stage shows the egg, and the
adult's address names it (`stage=adult`). The tool refuses any other opening stage.
One stage has since been given a word: the fertilised egg, **planned**, the same day, after the
engine's lead confirmed that the record supports it. Its record is D141 (Xenopus cleavage is the
division work's first subject), D144, the cell-division plan, that work's design note and
PROGRESS's entry of 12 November 2026, in which the record's first prediction for the egg, J1, made
with an independent reference model, fails against the measured membrane potential. The ladder's
next word, "designed", would be met too (a design note is on main), and the file states the lower
word, as `schema/ENTRIES.md` allows; the text says what the lower word leaves out. The tool refuses
a word on any other stage, any other word on the egg, and any other record.

- **The egg's size** is a review's: Sato (2025), Biomolecules 16(1):22 (doi:10.3390/biom16010022,
  CC BY 4.0), section 2.1, paragraph 1, gives the frog's externally fertilised eggs a diameter of
  about 1.2 mm. The egg is drawn as a sphere of that size, ending at its cell.
- **What is drawn inside the egg is not the egg's.** Its parts are the generic cell's, those of the
  skin-like cell drawn for the adult, enlarged to the egg's size; an egg's yolk, its pigment and
  the rest of what it holds are not drawn, and the words on the picture say so.
- **A named stage** has no size under usable terms; it stands on the strip with what is missing,
  and cannot be entered. No length of a tadpole was found under usable terms (the landmarks table
  of Zahn 2022 is a supplement whose record states no licence of its own, so it was not read).
- The build reads each paper's licence and DOI in its full text and the words of its running text
  that each name and number rests on, and stops if one is gone or the block gives another number.
  The papers' records are Europe PMC's, from the gathering round's searches.

## The proteome, and the eight rules

UniProt's reference proteome UP000186698 (release 2026_04, strain J_2021) holds 62,048 entries
for 36,429 genes: 3,523 reviewed and 58,525 unreviewed. Its gene-by-gene set holds 36,429
entries, all 3,523 reviewed ones among them.

1. **Both copies of a gene are shown, each as its own protein.** The set lists the copy on an L
   chromosome and the copy on an S chromosome as two genes, and nothing read here says they are
   one. No pair is merged.
2. **A reviewed entry wins over unreviewed twins of its gene.** A gene is the entries tied
   together by the NCBI gene identifiers UniProt's cross-references give them (an entry may carry
   several). Where a gene has a reviewed entry and unreviewed entries among the set's, only the
   reviewed one is shown: **1,425 unreviewed entries of 1,410 genes are not shown**, and
   **35,004 proteins are shown**, 3,523 reviewed and 31,481 unreviewed. The build stops if these
   counts differ from those measured when the rule was decided. Four genes have two reviewed
   entries; each names the gene's twins, so two twins are named twice.
3. **Every protein is marked with its copy,** read from UniProt's proteome component of its entry,
   never from its gene's symbol (where a reviewed entry wins it carries a plain name, `rpa2-b`,
   so the symbol would lose the copy where the entry is best).
4. **A twin that is not shown stays findable.** The reviewed entry names each one (`mvp.L
   (A0A8J0U5T9)` on Q6PF69, `mvp`) in its mark `same_gene`; the names file carries the mark, the
   page's search finds the shown protein by the twin's gene name or accession and says so, and
   the protein's panel lists them as "Unreviewed entries of its gene, not shown".
5. **What remains is said:** 476 genes still stand twice or more, with 952 unreviewed entries and
   no reviewed one, since no record read here picks between them.
6. **The gene symbols are UniProt's** (CC BY 4.0). UniProt's entries name Xenbase's nomenclature
   as the source of the frog's gene names; 11,361 of the 35,004 proteins shown carry such a name,
   as written here. It was decided on 19 November 2026 that the names are used as read from
   UniProt, with Xenbase named behind them; nothing else of Xenbase is used (below).
   **Placeholder names are not written.** 304 gene names and 49 protein names of the
   proteins shown, and the gene names of 4 twins not shown, are not written here: UniProt gives
   them, and they are placeholder names of Xenbase's nomenclature that embed the gene's number
   there (a word of XB and four or more digits, with or without .L or .S). A field that holds one
   is left out whole, never rewritten, and such a protein is shown by its accession and found by
   it; a search by such a name finds nothing. This was decided on 19 November 2026: the user
   left the choice to the viewer's coordinator, who settled it with a second reviewer. Such a
   name is Xenbase's own number for the gene standing in a name's place, the tree writes no
   Xenbase identifier, and leaving it out can be undone where writing it into a public
   repository cannot. One switch in the tool (`WRITE_PLACEHOLDER_NAMES`, False) holds it; True
   would write the names as UniProt gives them.
7. **The NCBI gene identifiers tie a gene's entries together while building only.** None is
   written as a field, a mark or a tie; 934 of the proteins shown carry none. 10,878 of the gene
   names written are UniProt's (CC BY 4.0) of the form LOC and a number; in 10,826 of them the
   number is the gene's own at NCBI, as UniProt's cross-reference gives it. They are written as
   gene names like any other.
8. **Nothing of another species changes.** The shared machinery takes the frog's values as
   parameters (members per file, limits), and every other species' tracked and published files
   came out the same bytes.

## Its side of the atlas

Of the 49 units of the law inventory, 43 have proteins of the same family in the frog; the other 6

source of orthology was read, and nothing is called an ortholog.

## What rests on non-commercial or unlicensed sources

The project's rule (D173, 15 November 2026) sorts every source into three classes.
**Permissive** (CC BY, CC0, or a plain statement that no restriction is placed): values and
derived data may enter, with attribution. **Non-commercial** (any CC BY-NC licence, a program
given out for academic use only, or data given out for research and education only): analysis
only. **No stated terms**: nothing enters. By the user's ruling of 15 November 2026 a value read in
a permissive database stands on that database's terms, and the tool or rule system behind it is
named and counted. Nothing was downloaded from these but a statement of terms, and no tool was run.

## Readings that are this file's own

- **Two places the human's table has no line for:** "Nematocyst" (the vocabulary: an organelle of
  nematoblast cells, "principally found in anemones and jellyfishes"), stated by rule in 5
  unreviewed entries, and "Host cell", a virus's host, in 2; both are passed over, as the mouse's
  "Host cell" is, and the entries' other statements or features decide.
- **A gene** is a connected set of the gene-by-gene set's entries joined by shared NCBI gene
  identifiers: 1,410 genes by that reading, where counting distinct identifiers would give 1,426,
  since 75 entries carry two or more.
- **The copy** `L and S` is given to an entry that UniProt places on chromosomes of both sets (132
  shown); `neither stated` to one placed on an unplaced scaffold or the mitochondrion. The words
  "L" and "S" are UniProt's chromosome names; no saved source was read for what the letters stand
  for, so the files do not spell them out.

## How to rebuild

```bash
python3 -I tools/build_xenopus_atlas.py build --datasets DATA      # DATA holds every species' downloads; it reads data/species/stages/xenopus.json
python3 -I tools/build_xenopus_atlas.py members --out DIR      # the page builder runs this itself
python3 tools/extract_architecture.py --strict
```

The downloads are the gathering round's, under the datasets folder's `xenopus/` (read only):
`uniprot-xenopus-reference-proteome` and `europepmc-stage-sources-e1`, with the human's folders
for InterPro, the Gene Ontology and UniProt's vocabulary.
