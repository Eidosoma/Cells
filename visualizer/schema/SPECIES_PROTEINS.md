# A species' own proteins, for the atlas's shelves

Status: **fixed for the viewer, 15 November 2026**, so that each species' data can be
made before the drawing is. The user, asked whether Syn3A's own membrane machines should
be drawn on a shelf of their own: "yes, let's have all the shelfs simmilar format inside
outside for syn3a and all of them - humans have a 100k ones so let's maybe think about
that last". And of the catalogues too large for one marker each: "we should show them all
but maybe cool expandable protein families, same for humans".

The protein atlas draws the machines Cells has laws for. A species' side of it
(`SPECIES_ATLAS.md`) says which of those machines the species has. This file is the other
half: **the species' own proteins**, whether or not Cells has a law for them, laid out the
same way for every species.

**One format for every species: a shelf is a membrane, and every protein sits outside
it, in it or inside it.** A bacterium has one shelf, its cell membrane. An animal cell
has several: the cell membrane, and one for each internal membrane the data can tell
apart.

This is the viewer's data. It is not the engine's protein catalogue, and it says nothing
about the engine except which proteins the atlas already draws as a unit.

## File

`data/proteins/<species id>/shelves.json`, generated by the species' build tool from its
catalogue table, the same bytes every time.

```json
{
  "format": "cells.visualizer.species_proteins@1",
  "species": "syn3a",
  "made": "2026-11-15",
  "from": "GenBank CP016816.2: 455 protein products",
  "shelves": [
    {"id": "cell", "label": "Cell membrane", "outside": "Outside the cell", "inside": "Inside the cell"}
  ],
  "groups": [
    {"id": "transport", "label": "Transport", "from": "the 2019 paper's functional class"}
  ],
  "columns": ["id", "label", "gene", "shelf", "side", "basis", "group", "length", "unit", "marks"],
  "proteins": [
    ["JCVISYN3A_0001", "Chromosomal replication initiator protein", "dnaA", "cell", "inside", "predicted", "replication", 450, null, {"essential": "essential", "seen": true}]
  ],
  "side_notes": {"predicted": "How a side was predicted, in one or two sentences, naming the predictor and its source."},
  "marks": {"essential": {"label": "Essentiality", "values": ["essential", "quasi-essential", "non-essential"], "measured": true, "source": "the name of an entry in sources"}},
  "counts": {"outside": 0, "in": 0, "inside": 0, "not known": 0},
  "sources": [{"name": "…", "url": "https://…", "licence": "…", "accessed": "2026-11-15", "holds": "what was taken"}]
}
```

## Rules

- **Complete.** One line for every protein of the species' frozen catalogue, in the
  catalogue's order. `id` is the catalogue's identifier, so a line can be looked up there.
- **`side`** says where the protein sits against its shelf's membrane:
  - `outside`: beyond the membrane, free or anchored to its outer face;
  - `in`: it spans the membrane;
  - `inside`: enclosed by the membrane, free or attached to its inner face;
  - `not known`: the sources do not say and nothing predicts it. Such a protein is drawn
    apart, never placed by guess.
- **`basis`** says how the side is known:
  - `stated`: a curated entry or a paper states it for this organism;
  - `predicted`: read from the sequence (membrane-spanning segments, an export signal);
    `side_notes.predicted` names the predictor and where its output came from;
  - `by function`: taken from what the protein is annotated to do;
  - `none`: with `not known`.
  A side that is predicted or taken from function is said to be so wherever it is shown.
- **`group`** is the district the protein stands in on its side of the shelf. Groups come
  from a source's own classes where one has them, and say which.
- **`unit`** is the inventory unit the atlas already draws for this protein, or null. A
  protein with a unit is drawn as that unit; every other protein is a plain marker,
  labelled as not modelled.
- **`marks`** are what else can be shown or filtered: whether it was seen in proteomics,
  its essentiality, a measured or estimated amount. Each mark is declared once, with its
  values, whether it is measured, and its source (the name of an entry in `sources`). A
  mark is included only where the source's terms allow a copy. Marks with a fixed meaning,
  used where the data has them:
  - `essential`, `seen`, `amount`: as above;
  - `evidence`: for a `stated` side, whether an experiment or a curator's inference
    stands behind it;
  - `where`: a named place within the side, such as an organelle of the cell, or "at the
    membrane" for a protein known to be at the membrane with its face not stated (its
    side is then `not known`, unless another basis places it);
  - `also`: another side that a source states for the same protein. `side` is then the
    one stated first;
  - `complex`: the assembly the protein is a member of.
- **`side_notes`** has an entry for each basis the file uses, under the basis's own word
  (`stated`, `predicted`, `by function`, `none`), saying in one or two sentences how it
  was applied.
- A mark's `values` is the list of its values, or `"number"` or `"text"` where they are
  free.
- **A group may carry `within`,** the wider class it belongs to, for a district whose own
  name does not read alone, and its `count` and `sides`.
- **Dependence on non-commercial sources is flagged** (the user's rule, D173): every
  source states its licence, and a side or a mark that rests on a source under a
  non-commercial licence, or on one that states no open licence, says so in its note.
  No table, trace or plot from such a source is in a file.
- A file may carry further keys of its own, such as the checks its tool ran. The drawing
  ignores what it does not know.
- **Size.** A species' file stays under 1 MB. A catalogue too large for that, or for one
  marker each to be read, is never cut down: it is shown whole, as families that open
  (below). Human is the last to be done.
- **Terms and honesty** are as in `SPECIES_ATLAS.md`: every source with its terms, no
  machine path, nothing copied from a source that forbids it, and no word that the engine
  models a protein beyond `unit`.

## Large catalogues: families that open

Where a species has more proteins than can be read as one marker each (the planarian's
18,882; human), **every protein is still shown, grouped into families that open**. A
family is one marker on its shelf, sized by how many proteins it holds; opening it shows
its members, each a protein of the catalogue.

`shelves.json` then holds `families` in place of `proteins`:

```json
{
  "family_columns": ["id", "label", "shelf", "side", "group", "count", "sides", "file", "at"],
  "families": [
    ["IPR000990", "Innexin", "cell", "in", "channels", 68, {"in": 61, "inside": 5, "not known": 2}, "members-03.json", [1200, 1268]]
  ],
  "no_family": {"label": "No family assigned", "count": 0, "sides": {}, "file": "members-09.json", "at": [0, 0]},
  "family_from": "How a protein is given its one family, and from which source.",
  "member_columns": ["id", "label", "gene", "side", "basis", "length", "unit", "marks"]
}
```

- **One family for each protein.** `family_from` says how it is chosen where a protein
  matches several, and which source names the families. A protein with no family is not
  dropped: it is listed under `no_family`, which opens like any other.
- **A family stands on the side where most of its members sit.** `sides` counts its
  members on each side, and each member keeps its own side and basis.
- **Members are cut into files** of a few thousand proteins, in the families' order, so
  that opening a family fetches one file. `file` and `at` say where a family's members
  are. The page builder writes those files beside the page from the catalogue table; they
  are not kept in the repository, where the table already is.
- A family's `group` is its district, as for a protein.

## In the page

The shelves are large, so they are not part of the scene. Each species' file is published
beside the page and fetched when its species is shown, as replays are. The scene carries
only, for each species, the file's name, its counts and its sources.
