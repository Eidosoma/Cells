# A species' own proteins, for the atlas's shelves

Status: **fixed for the viewer, 15 November 2026**, so that each species' data can be
made before the drawing is. The user, asked whether Syn3A's own membrane machines should
be drawn on a shelf of their own: "yes, let's have all the shelfs simmilar format inside
outside for syn3a and all of them - humans have a 100k ones so let's maybe think about
that last". And of the catalogues too large for one marker each: "we should show them all
but maybe cool expandable protein families, same for humans".

The protein atlas draws the machines Cells has laws for. A species' side of it
(`SPECIES_ATLAS.md`) says which of those machines the species has. This file is the other
half: **the species' own proteins**, whether or not Cells has a law for them, laid out the
same way for every species.

**One format for every species: a shelf is a membrane, and every protein sits outside
it, in it or inside it.** A bacterium has one shelf, its cell membrane. An animal cell
has several: the cell membrane, and one for each internal membrane the data can tell
apart.

This is the viewer's data. It is not the engine's protein catalogue, and it says nothing
about the engine except which proteins the atlas already draws as a unit.

## File

`data/proteins/<species id>/shelves.json`, generated by the species' build tool from its
catalogue table, the same bytes every time.

```json
{
  "format": "cells.visualizer.species_proteins@1",
  "species": "syn3a",
  "made": "2026-11-15",
  "from": "GenBank CP016816.2: 455 protein products",
  "shelves": [
    {"id": "cell", "label": "Cell membrane", "outside": "Outside the cell", "inside": "Inside the cell"}
  ],
  "groups": [
    {"id": "transport", "label": "Transport", "from": "the 2019 paper's functional class"}
  ],
  "columns": ["id", "label", "gene", "shelf", "side", "basis", "group", "length", "unit", "marks"],
  "proteins": [
    ["JCVISYN3A_0001", "Chromosomal replication initiator protein", "dnaA", "cell", "inside", "predicted", "replication", 450, null, {"essential": "essential", "seen": true}]
  ],
  "side_notes": {"predicted": "How a side was predicted, in one or two sentences, naming the predictor and its source."},
  "marks": {"essential": {"label": "Essentiality", "values": ["essential", "quasi-essential", "non-essential"], "measured": true, "source": "the name of an entry in sources"}},
  "counts": {"outside": 0, "in": 0, "inside": 0, "not known": 0},
  "sources": [{"name": "…", "url": "https://…", "licence": "…", "class": "permissive", "accessed": "2026-11-15", "holds": "what was taken"}]
}
```

## Rules

- **Complete.** One line for every protein of the species' frozen catalogue, in the
  catalogue's order. `id` is the catalogue's identifier, so a line can be looked up there.
- **`side`** says where the protein sits against its shelf's membrane:
  - `outside`: beyond the membrane, free or anchored to its outer face;
  - `in`: it spans the membrane;
  - `inside`: enclosed by the membrane, free or attached to its inner face;
  - `not known`: the sources do not say and nothing predicts it. Such a protein is drawn
    apart, never placed by guess.
- **`shelf`** is the id of one of the file's shelves (or null, where the section on a
  curated proteome allows it). A family, and a part, has at most one line on a shelf.
- **`basis`** says how the side is known:
  - `stated`: a curated entry or a paper states it for this organism;
  - `predicted`: read from the sequence (membrane-spanning segments, an export signal);
    `side_notes.predicted` names the predictor and where its output came from;
  - `by function`: taken from what the protein is annotated to do;
  - `none`: with `not known`.
  A side that is predicted or taken from function is said to be so wherever it is shown.
- **`group`** is the district the protein stands in on its side of the shelf. Groups come
  from a source's own classes where one has them, and say which.
- **`predicted`** (optional, true or false) says whether every protein of the catalogue
  is itself a prediction, from a genome or from transcripts, with none measured. The page
  then calls each a predicted protein and its side a prediction on a predicted protein.
- **`unit`** is the inventory unit the atlas already draws for this protein, or null. A
  protein with a unit is drawn as that unit; every other protein is a plain marker,
  labelled as not modelled.
- **`marks`** are what else can be shown or filtered: whether it was seen in proteomics,
  its essentiality, a measured or estimated amount. Each mark is declared once, with its
  values, whether it is measured, and its source (the name of an entry in `sources`). A
  mark is included only where the source's terms allow a copy. Marks with a fixed meaning,
  used where the data has them:
  - `essential`, `seen`, `amount`: as above;
  - `evidence`: for a `stated` side, whether an experiment or a curator's inference
    stands behind it;
  - `where`: a named place within the side, such as an organelle of the cell, or "at the
    membrane" for a protein known to be at the membrane with its face not stated (its
    side is then `not known`, unless another basis places it);
  - `also`: another side that a source states for the same protein. `side` is then the
    one stated first;
  - `complex`: the assembly the protein is a member of.
- **`side_notes`** has an entry for each basis the file uses, under the basis's own word
  (`stated`, `predicted`, `by function`, `none`), saying in one or two sentences how it
  was applied.
- A mark's `values` is the list of its values, or `"number"` or `"text"` where they are
  free.
- **A group may carry `within`,** the wider class it belongs to, for a district whose own
  name does not read alone, and its `count` and `sides`.
- **A tool or rule system behind a value is named, with `through`.** Where a side, a
  mark or a note rests on what a program or a rule system of another owner computed and a
  database gives out (the helices TMHMM predicts, as WormBase ParaSite ran it; a place
  that a UniProt entry states by a PROSITE or a HAMAP rule), that program or rule system
  is a source of the file in its own right:
  - `name`, `url`, `licence` and `class` are its own: its terms as its own page states
    them, read and saved, never classed from memory. A program given out for academic use
    only is `non-commercial`. A page that could not be read gives the `licence` "Terms not
    read: …" and the class `no stated terms`.
  - **`through` stands on the tool's entry and names the database:** the `name` of the
    source of the same file in whose records its results were read, or several such names
    joined by " and ". That database is permissive and has no `through` of its own.
    `through` is never put on the database's entry to name the tool.
  - `holds` begins "Nothing was downloaded from it" and says what rests on it, with counts.
  - A mark's `source` names the database its values were taken from; it may name the tool
    beside it.
  - **A value stands on the terms of the database it was read from, and the tool behind
    it is named and counted** (the user's ruling of 15 November 2026: the project's rule,
    not a legal opinion). It holds for every species: asked the same day whether it does,
    the user said "yes it holds for this open source project". So where the tool's class
    is not permissive, the value stays.
    The note beside the file lists the tool with its terms and its counts, and
    `tests/licence_classes.test.mjs` keeps it in a register under its species; one the
    register does not hold fails the test, so that a new one is noticed and put to the
    user. A permissive one (HAMAP) needs no listing.
- **Every source has a `class`, and a file holds no value from a source that is not
  permissive** (D173, 15 November 2026). `class` is one of `permissive` (CC BY, CC0, or
  a plain statement that no restriction is placed on use), `non-commercial` (a CC BY-NC
  licence of any kind) and `no stated terms`. A side, a group and a mark rest on
  permissive sources alone, and a mark's `source` names one. A source of another class
  is listed in one of two ways only: as a pointer, whose `holds` says that nothing is
  taken from it and what is to be read there; or, where a permissive source gives out
  results that originate in it (a database's record of another database's signature),
  with `through`, the name of that permissive source, and a `holds` that says nothing
  was downloaded from it and what rests on it, as the bullet above sets out. Such a
  value may stay: by the user's ruling of 15 November 2026 it stands on the terms of the
  database it was read from, and the tool behind it is named and counted, in the file
  and in the test's register. `tests/licence_classes.test.mjs` holds every file to it.
- A file may carry further keys of its own, such as the checks its tool ran. The drawing
  ignores what it does not know.
- **Size.** A species' file stays under 1 MB. A catalogue too large for that, or for one
  marker each to be read, is never cut down: it is shown whole, as families that open
  (below). Human is the last to be done.
- **Terms and honesty** are as in `SPECIES_ATLAS.md`: every source with its terms, no
  machine path, nothing copied from a source that forbids it, and no word that the engine
  models a protein beyond `unit`.

## Large catalogues: families that open

Where a species has more proteins than can be read as one marker each (the planarian's
18,882; human), **every protein is still shown, grouped into families that open**. A
family is one marker on its shelf, sized by how many proteins it holds; opening it shows
its members, each a protein of the catalogue.

`shelves.json` then holds `families` in place of `proteins`:

```json
{
  "family_columns": ["id", "label", "shelf", "side", "group", "count", "sides", "file", "at"],
  "families": [
    ["IPR000990", "Innexin", "cell", "in", "channels", 68, {"in": 61, "inside": 5, "not known": 2}, "members-03.json", [1200, 1268]]
  ],
  "no_family": {"label": "No family assigned", "count": 0, "sides": {}, "file": "members-09.json", "at": [0, 0]},
  "family_from": "How a protein is given its one family, and from which source.",
  "member_columns": ["id", "label", "gene", "side", "basis", "length", "unit", "marks"]
}
```

- **One family for each protein.** `family_from` says how it is chosen where a protein
  matches several, and which source names the families. A protein with no family is not
  dropped: it is listed under `no_family`, which opens like any other.
- **A family stands on the side where most of its members sit.** `sides` counts its
  members on each side, and each member keeps its own side and basis.
- **Members are cut into files** of a few thousand proteins, in the families' order, so
  that opening a family fetches one file. `file` and `at` say where a family's members
  are. The page builder writes those files beside the page from the catalogue table; they
  are not kept in the repository, where the table already is.
- A family's `group` is its district, as for a protein.
- **Further family columns** may follow the nine, declared in `family_columns`: `units`,
  the inventory units whose products the family holds, and `where`, a hint at which
  membrane the family belongs to. A member's `unit` is then a list, or null.
- **`no_family` may be given in parts.** Where a broader grouping is honest (a
  superfamily, or "a domain only", or "no signature at all"), `no_family` carries
  `parts_from`, `part_columns` and `parts`, each part with its own `file` and `at`, and no
  `file` of its own. A part is never called a family.
- **One shelf may stand for several membranes.** Where the data can say that a protein
  spans a membrane but not which, the species has one shelf, labelled as standing for the
  cell's membranes together, and its `outside` says what "beyond a membrane" then covers.
- **The members' file** written beside the page has the format
  `cells.visualizer.species_protein_members@1` and holds `columns` and `members`.
- **`by_basis`**, for each side how many proteins have it by each basis, is given by
  every catalogue in families: the page's words on how sides are known rest on it.

## A curated proteome: several shelves, places as stated

Where curators state where a protein is (the human proteome, from UniProt's reviewed
entries), the species has several shelves, one for each membrane the data tells apart,
stacked in the page. Fixed on 15 November 2026, before the human data was made.

- **The shelves** are, at least: `cell` (the cell membrane), `er` (the endoplasmic
  reticulum's membrane), `mito` (the mitochondrion's inner membrane), `nucleus` (the
  nuclear envelope), and `other`, one shelf for the cell's remaining membranes together
  (Golgi, endosomes, lysosomes, peroxisomes, vesicles, the mitochondrion's outer
  membrane), each protein there naming its compartment in the mark `where`. A further
  shelf of its own is given only to a membrane that holds enough proteins to be read as
  one; the file's note says which and why.
- **A protein's shelf and side come from its stated location:**
  - in a membrane (a protein that spans it): that membrane's shelf, side `in`;
  - within a compartment (the ER's lumen, the mitochondrial matrix, the nucleus): that
    compartment's shelf, side `inside`;
  - in the cytoplasm or the cytosol, free or on the cytoplasmic face of the cell membrane:
    shelf `cell`, side `inside`;
  - secreted, in the extracellular space or matrix, or anchored to the cell membrane's
    outer face: shelf `cell`, side `outside`;
  - between the mitochondrion's two membranes: shelf `mito`, side `outside`;
  - on the cytoplasmic face of an internal membrane: that membrane's shelf, side
    `outside`, since the cytoplasm lies beyond it.
- **Several stated locations:** the first one stated decides the shelf and the side; the
  others go in the mark `also`, in the same words.
- **`basis`** is `stated`, and the mark `evidence` says what stands behind the statement:
  an experiment, a curator's inference or similarity, or an automatic rule.
- **No stated location:** `predicted` where the entry carries a predicted membrane
  segment or export signal, with the predictor listed among the sources as the rule on a
  tool behind a value asks (its own entry, whose `through` names the database);
  otherwise `not known`, set apart.
- **`unit`** is set by exact accession: the inventory's own candidate products name their
  human entries.
- **One marker stands for one canonical protein.** Its isoforms are a count in the mark
  `isoforms`; unreviewed entries are not listed, and the file's `from` says how many of
  each there are.
- **Families** are InterPro's, by the rule the planarian's file uses, so that a family has
  the same id in every species.
- **A family has one line for each shelf that holds members of it,** so that a marker on
  a shelf holds only proteins of that shelf, each shelf's count is what stands on it, and
  an opened family is drawn against one membrane. `id` and `label` are the family's own,
  the same on every shelf; `shelf` says which; `side`, `group`, `count`, `sides`, `file`,
  `at` and `units` are of its members on that shelf alone. A line is identified by `id`
  and `shelf` together, and a member's shelf is its line's. The parts of `no_family`
  likewise, with `shelf` among `part_columns`. The file gives the number of distinct
  families as well as the number of lines.
- **A membrane that is not named has a shelf of its own,** last in order: `membrane`,
  "A membrane, not told which". It holds the proteins stated to be in a membrane with no
  membrane named, and those with no stated location and a predicted membrane segment. A
  protein with no stated location, no predicted segment and a predicted export signal
  stands on that shelf's `outside`, whose text says what lies beyond a membrane there:
  outside the cell, or within a compartment. Such proteins are never put on `other`,
  which is for membranes that are named.
- **A protein is counted and drawn with its shelf; one with no shelf stands apart from
  them all.** A protein whose compartment is stated but not its side (stated to be in the
  mitochondrion with no part of it named, or at a membrane with its face not told) is on
  that compartment's shelf with side `not known` and the place in `where`. Its basis is
  `none`, as for any side that is not known, while `evidence` still says what stands
  behind the stated compartment. It counts for that shelf and is drawn set apart beside
  it. A protein with no stated location and nothing predicted has no shelf: its `shelf`
  is null and its side `not known`, and a family has one line with a null shelf for such
  members. It counts for no shelf and stands in the page's one set-apart area, in front
  of every shelf.
- **A family of one has no line.** A family with a single member in the whole catalogue
  does not open, and a line for each would make the file too large (the human's 5,164
  families of one would bring it to 1.4 MB). Its protein is listed under `lone`, which
  is shaped like `no_family` in parts: one part for each shelf, district and side
  (`kind` `lone`), opening straight to its proteins, each of which names its family in
  its mark `family`. A part of `lone` is never called a family. Counts are over the
  families' lines, the parts of `lone` and the parts of `no_family` together.
  `distinct_families` counts the families that have lines, and `families_in_all` those
  of one as well. The members follow the families' lines, then the parts of `lone`, then
  the parts of `no_family`.
- **Each line of a family carries `total`,** its family's members on every shelf and on
  none, so that the drawing can let a family of five or more stand on its own on a shelf
  where it has fewer.
- **Where the first stated place names no side,** a later statement of the same entry
  that names a part of that place and its side settles it, as `stated`. Failing that, a
  membrane segment in the entry, where one membrane is meant, or a signal peptide with
  no segment, settles it as `predicted`. The file counts how many proteins each rule
  places (`placed_by`).
- **Marks of a curated proteome.** `seen` is there the database's level of evidence that
  the protein exists, and its label says so; `where` is the database's own words for the
  place; `family` names the family of a protein listed under `lone`.
- **Counts on several shelves.** `counts` stays the four sides over the whole catalogue.
  `shelf_counts` gives the same four for each shelf, under the shelf's id, and under
  `no shelf` for the proteins on none. `distinct_families` gives the number of families,
  where `families` has more lines than that. Each is checked against the lines.
- **A shelf may give `in`,** how a protein in its membrane is said ("In a membrane of the
  Golgi or of a vesicle"), where its label does not read within a sentence. A label reads
  without it when it is a singular name ("Nuclear envelope"), a plural ending in
  "membranes", or begins with "A".

## In the page

The shelves are large, so they are not part of the scene. Each species' file is published
beside the page and fetched when its species is shown, as replays are. The scene carries
only, for each species, the file's name, its counts and its sources.
