# Syn3A proteins: the start of a catalogue

`proteins.tsv` lists the 455 protein products of one frozen genome annotation of the
minimal cell JCVI-syn3A, one per line, with what open sources say of each: what it is
named, how long it is, what it is for, whether the cell can do without it, and how many
copies were estimated in a cell.

`shelves.json` says, for the same 455 proteins, where each sits against the cell
membrane and how that is known. It is described in its own section below.

Generated by `tools/build_syn3a_atlas.py`; do not edit. This is reference data for the
viewer. It is not the engine's protein catalogue, and nothing in the engine reads it.

## The frozen annotation

GenBank **CP016816.2**: "Synthetic bacterium JCVI-Syn3A, complete genome", 543,379 base pairs, one circular
chromosome, annotation of 12 April 2018 (taxon 2144189). It is the annotation that the
2019 paper names and that its tables are keyed to, so every column below can be tied to a
locus tag of this record.

| In the record | Count | In this table |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coding sequences with a protein product | 455 | all 455 |
| of which kept from *Mycoplasma mycoides* | 452 | `origin` is `minimal genome` |
| of which added for genetic manipulation (tetM and the yeast genes HIS3 and MET14) | 3 | `origin` is `added for manipulation` |
| Pseudogenes, with no protein product (JCVISYN3A_0051, JCVISYN3A_0546, JCVISYN3A_0602) | 3 | not listed |
| RNA genes | 38 | not listed |

**Why 452 and 455 are both right.** Papers on this organism count 452 protein-coding
genes; the record holds 455 protein products. The difference is the 3 genes added
for genetic manipulation, which the 2019 paper's own table sets apart. The record's 496
genes are these 455, the 3 pseudogenes and the 38 RNA genes.

UniProtKB holds no entry of this organism. UniProt's archive of sequences (UniParc) holds
exactly these 455 sequences, which is where the family signatures come from.

Counts, as built:

- **Essentiality** of the 452 kept genes: 270 essential, 113 quasi-essential, 69 non-essential.
  The paper's Table 1 totals 271, 113 and 68: one gene differs between that table and the
  supplementary file, and the file is what is copied here.
- **Detected:** 428 of the 455: 427 of the 452 kept and 1 of the 3 added (tetM). The
  estimated copies of the detected proteins sum to 77,028.
- **Identical sequence in another organism's UniProtKB entry:** 45 of the 455.
- **No signature at all:** 46 of the 455.

What is not here: sequences (they stay with the downloads), simulated counts from
any model, and structures. Where a protein sits is not in this table either: it is in
`shelves.json`, described below. Strain
identity matters: every value is for JCVI-syn3A itself. Nothing is carried over from
JCVI-syn3.0, JCVI-syn3B or *Mycoplasma mycoides*, except that `uniprotkb_identical` names
other organisms' entries and says so.

## `shelves.json`: where each protein sits

For the shelves of the protein atlas (`schema/SPECIES_PROTEINS.md`): one line for each of
the 455 proteins, in this table's order, saying which side of the cell membrane it sits
on, how that is known, and the district it stands in. The organism has one membrane, so
there is one shelf.

| Side | Proteins | How it is known |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outside the cell, anchored to the membrane's outer face | 13 | 13 predicted |
| In the membrane, spanning it | 86 | 86 predicted |
| Inside the cell, free or attached to the membrane's inner face | 352 | 6 stated, 326 predicted, 20 by function |
| Not known | 4 | 4 nothing settles it |

**How a side is known.**

- **Stated (6).** A reviewed UniProtKB entry with exactly this protein's
  sequence states the place. Every such entry is of another organism, so it states where
  the same protein sits there; the entry is named with the protein. What stands behind
  the statements: similarity to another protein for 1 and a family rule for 5; none rests on an experiment.
  Such an entry also says that potA is at the cell membrane as a peripheral protein, without saying
  which face. The reading places it inside, and it is marked as at the membrane.
- **Predicted (425).** This tool's own reading of the sequence. It is
  not the output of an established predictor: UniProt's archive holds family signatures on
  these sequences and no prediction of membrane-spanning stretches, and no UniProtKB entry
  of any organism with one of these sequences gives one.
  One unreviewed entry of another organism (A0AAQ3DPT5), with the sequence of rpmF, carries
  an automatic lipid-anchor mark from the PROSITE profile used below; it is set aside there.
  - *In the membrane:* a stretch of 19 residues whose mean hydropathy on Kyte and
    Doolittle's scale is 1.6 or more and which holds no charged residue, or
    2.2 or more with at most one. The window and the 1.6 are the usual
    reading of that scale (Paulet and others 2011 state it). The conditions on
    charged residues are this tool's, set against the checks below.
  - *Outside:* the lipoprotein signature (PROSITE PS51257, as UniProt's archive
    gives it) from the first residue to a cysteine, with a hydrophobic stretch before the
    cysteine. The lipoproteins of mycoplasmas are bound to the membrane and exposed on the
    cell's surface (Christodoulides and others 2018); that is said of mycoplasmas in general
    and was not measured in this strain.
  - *Inside:* no such stretch and no lipid anchor. This includes proteins attached to the
    membrane's inner face, such as the ATP-binding parts of transporters.
- **By function (20).** The sequence has a hydrophobic stretch, but the
  protein's class in the 2019 paper leaves no doubt that it works inside the cell:
  translation, transcription, the upkeep of DNA, sugar breakdown, the pentose phosphate
  pathway or nucleotide metabolism. Function is never used to place a transport protein.
- **Not known (4).** A hydrophobic stretch that holds charged residues, in a
  protein of unknown function: JCVISYN3A_0286, JCVISYN3A_0326, JCVISYN3A_0373, JCVISYN3A_0495. They are not placed.

**How the reading fares against what is known.**

- **Proteins that work inside the cell.** Of the 224 proteins in the classes named under
  "By function", 20 have a stretch at 1.6 or more, which the usual reading alone would call
  membrane-spanning. With the condition on charged residues the tool reads 1 of them so
  (ldh); it too is placed inside, by its function.
- **Proteins named as membrane-spanning.** All 13 proteins that the annotation calls a
  permease are read as membrane-spanning.
- **Stated places.** The reading agrees with all 6.
- **The lipoprotein signature** matches 15 sequences. 14 are kept: 13 are placed outside, and
  1, with a membrane-spanning stretch after the anchor, in the membrane. Set aside, for
  want of a hydrophobic stretch before the cysteine: rpmF.
- **Against the literature.** Here 86 proteins span the membrane and 13 are anchored on it,
  99 together. 89 of them were seen in the proteomics, with an estimated 7,685 of
  the 77,028 copies. The 2022 whole-cell model gives its own count of membrane
  proteins and of their copies. That paper is under a non-commercial licence, so its
  numbers are not repeated here and no list of the model's was copied or compared: the
  comparison is to be read there.

**Marks** kept with each protein: essentiality and whether it was seen (both measured),
the estimated copies (`amount`), the three proteins added for manipulation, the finer
class, the number of membrane-spanning stretches read, the lipid anchor, what stands
behind a stated side, and its assembly where the record's product name gives it outright
(ATP synthase 8, RNA polymerase 5, ribosome 52).

**What it does not say.** It gives no topology: not which end of a membrane protein faces
out, and not which face a protein inside is attached to. The count of stretches can fall
short, since a stretch that holds charged residues is not read. Of the 86 proteins placed
in the membrane, 20 have one stretch only, and for 6 of those it is at the very start
of the protein: that may be an export signal that is cut off, and the protein would then
sit outside. They are marked. It marks no assembly beyond the three named. `unit` is
empty throughout, because the atlas found none of the law inventory's units in this
organism.

## How to rebuild

```bash
python3 tools/build_syn3a_atlas.py fetch --evidence DIR --datasets DATA   # network; saves what is not yet there
python3 tools/build_syn3a_atlas.py build --evidence DIR --datasets DATA   # offline; the same bytes every time
```

`DATA` holds the downloads, one folder per source with a MANIFEST.json; `DIR` holds the
log of what this tool asked. Both are kept outside the repository. The build writes this
folder's three files, `data/species/syn3a.json` and `data/proteins/atlases/syn3a.json`.
`docs/SYN3A.md` describes the method and its limits.
