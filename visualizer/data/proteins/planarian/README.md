# Planarian proteins

Three things are recorded here for the planarian *Schmidtea mediterranea*.

1. **The mass-spectrometry library of about 10,000 proteins was not imported.** It was
   found, its terms were read, and its list of proteins could not be reached under terms
   that allow a copy. What was found and what is missing is set out below.
2. **`predicted_proteins.tsv` is the open set, brought in whole in the meantime** (the user's
   decision, 14 November 2026): the 18,882 proteins on open record for the species,
   17,294 gene models of one genome annotation and 1,588 UniProtKB entries. They are
   predicted from the genome and from transcripts, not measured. It is not the library and
   does not stand in for it.
3. **`reference_proteins.tsv` holds the 229 of those lines that the planarian protein atlas
   draws on:** the 227 proteins it names and 2 more that are joined to them.

Generated by `tools/build_planarian_atlas.py`; do not edit. This is reference data for
the viewer's protein atlas. It is not the engine's protein catalogue, and nothing in the
engine reads it.

## `predicted_proteins.tsv`

18,882 lines, each one protein, tab-separated under a header line: 17,294 gene models
with a predicted protein, then 1,588 UniProtKB entries. The columns are explained in the
next section.

- **Not measured.** A gene model is a prediction from the genome. Of the UniProtKB
  entries, UniProt marks 1,562 "evidence at transcript level", 14 "inferred from homology",
  11 "predicted" and 1 "evidence at protein level"; the `note` column gives each
  entry's level.
- **Not the animal's proteome.** The gene models are those of one haplotype of one genome
  assembly, and the entries overlap them: 1,288 entries are joined to a gene model
  (column `match`), so one protein can stand on two lines.
- **No sequences.** They stay with the saved responses, outside the repository.
- **Nothing here says which of these proteins the animal makes, where or when.** That is
  what the mass-spectrometry library measures.

## `reference_proteins.tsv`

229 proteins, one per line: 210 gene models and 19 UniProtKB entries.
227 are named by `data/proteins/atlases/planarian.json`; the other 2 are entries or
gene models joined to one that is. Tab-separated, with a header line. Each line is a line
of `predicted_proteins.tsv`.

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `db` | `wormbase_parasite` for a gene model, `uniprot` for a UniProtKB entry |
| `id` | The gene identifier in WormBase ParaSite, or the UniProtKB accession |
| `length` | Residues: of the gene's longest predicted protein, or of the entry |
| `kind` | `gene model`, `reviewed entry` or `unreviewed entry` |
| `name` | For a gene model, the InterPro families its signatures name (empty when none does); for an entry, UniProt's protein name |
| `gene` | Gene names of a UniProtKB entry, as submitted |
| `interpro` | InterPro signatures: on any predicted protein of the gene, as WormBase ParaSite computed them for its release; or on the entry, from UniProt |
| `match` | The other side of a join: a gene model's UniProtKB entries, or an entry's gene model |
| `note` | For a gene model, its longest predicted protein and how many more it has (`+2`); for an entry, UniProt's level of evidence |

The atlas's proteins were drawn from all 17,294 gene models and 1,588 UniProtKB entries, which
are the lines of `predicted_proteins.tsv`.

A gene model is a prediction from one haplotype of one genome assembly
(schMedS3_haplotype1, strain S2F19, annotation version 2022-10; BioProject
PRJNA885486, registered 2022-09-29 by the Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences).
WormBase ParaSite counts 21,392 coding genes in it; the 17,294 searched are those with a
predicted protein (it marks the other 4,098 as not translating). Sequences are not
in either file.

The database's page of this annotation names no paper. That it is the annotation this paper describes is inferred, not stated by either: the assembly has the paper's name (schMedS3 haplotype 1), the count of coding genes in the second haplotype is the same in both (21,310), and in the first it is 21,392 here against the paper's 21,401.

An entry and a gene model are joined when at least 80% of the entry's 8-residue
words occur in the model's predicted protein and no other gene reaches half of that
share; 1,288 of the 1,588 entries join a gene model this way. A join says the two are
copies of one sequence, not that either is confirmed in the animal.

## How to rebuild

```bash
python3 tools/build_planarian_atlas.py fetch --evidence DIR   # network; saves every response
python3 tools/build_planarian_atlas.py build --evidence DIR   # offline; the same bytes every time
```

`DIR` holds the saved responses and `requests.json`, the record of what was asked and
when; it is kept outside the repository. The build also writes
`data/proteins/atlases/planarian.json`. `docs/PLANARIAN_ATLAS.md` describes the method
and its limits.
