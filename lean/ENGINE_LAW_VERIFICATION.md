## What existing Lean projects establish

- **Biological definitions.** Jones and Josephson proved properties of transcription and translation in Lean 4. Trying to prove that complementary coding and template strands yield the same RNA exposed an error in their definitions. Their published decoder also maps an unrecognized input to STOP. For Cells, the lesson is to state what counts as an admissible input and to check a relationship between two independently meaningful quantities. [Genetics preprint, especially §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36343v1) · [Decoder source](https://github.com/ATOMSLab/LeanGenetics/blob/ddb1752e32e8ce2230fd453e56ec3460fcad361e/GeneticCode/GeneticCode.lean)
- **Reaction accounting.** VeriBiota gives a worked Lean proof that a reaction firing preserves a weighted total when the products and reactants have equal weight. Its example preserves total population during infection and recovery. The Lean 4 chemical reaction network library `crnt-lean` proves that the velocity of a mass-action trajectory lies in the span of its reaction vectors. The same vectors determine how Cells books reaction extents. [VeriBiota invariants](https://omnisgenomics.github.io/VeriBiota/invariants/) · [CRNT conservation theorem](https://github.com/marpaia/crnt-lean/blob/7386777488a41efd92e6abc2bccf889fb611660e/CRNT/Dynamics/ConservationLaw.lean#L46)
- **Chemical laws and numerical evidence.** Bobbin and colleagues formalized adsorption and gas-law derivations, making assumptions such as nonzero divisors explicit. Their published code uses Lean 3. A later Lean 4 project, LeanBET, uses one algorithm over real numbers for proofs and floating point for computations. Its authors report machine-precision agreement with the Python reference on 18 of 19 adsorption datasets and a 0.03% difference on the nineteenth. That is a precedent for reporting a theorem alongside a measured discrepancy. [Chemical physics paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.12150v5) · [Authors’ code and version](https://atomslab.github.io/LeanChemicalTheories/) · [LeanBET paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16169)

## Reusing chemical physics proofs

The Lean charter describes how to choose between a direct import, an optional comparison and a local proof. Worked examples include the units of electrode work, the ideal mixing term and a two-state equilibrium probability. [Charter](AGENTS.md) · [Proofs and build commands](README.md#reusing-a-proof)

Bobbin and colleagues’ Digital Discovery article and arXiv:2210.12150 report the same work. Their Lean 3.51.1 Langmuir theorem derives site occupancy from balanced association and dissociation rates. For the one-ligand, two-form case of Cells’ rapid binding law, the analogous dimensionless ratio is free concentration divided by Kd. Two short Lean 4 proofs connect that kinetic balance and the Cells form weights to the same occupancy. The biological claim remains open for measured binding curves. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3DD00077J) · [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12150) · [Archived toolchain](https://atomslab.github.io/LeanChemicalTheories/) · [Upstream theorem](https://atomslab.github.io/LeanChemicalTheories/adsorption/langmuir_kinetics.html#langmuir_single_site_kinetic_derivation) · [Cells binding claim](claims/single-site-binding.md)

Chojecki’s survey discusses how units and comparisons with numerical solvers complement a real-arithmetic theorem. The face-power proof checks the factor of 0.001 from the SI prefixes in the default build. An optional check imports two dimensional-analysis modules from ATOMS Lab’s Lean 4.23.0-rc2 package. Their proofs compile on Cells’ 4.19.0 pin with a one-line change to a Mathlib import path in a temporary build copy. The dimensional identity cancels amount when flux is multiplied by molar potential. [Survey](https://www.ulam.ai/research/lean-science.pdf) · [Pinned package](https://github.com/ATOMSLab/LeanDimensionalAnalysis/tree/de263eed945693058ef2b8a1fa56c2ec5642ea7a) · [Units check](checks/units/FacePower.lean) · [Reuse and build instructions](README.md#reusing-a-proof)

## Chemistry libraries tested against Cells

On 19 November 2026 we compared three upstream libraries with equations already used by Cells. The two optional imports below run on our Lean and Mathlib 4.19.0 pins. The default build still uses Mathlib alone. [Cells toolchain](lean-toolchain) · [Dependency revisions](lake-manifest.json)

**Hill activation and inhibition.** The MIT-licensed `grn-lean` uses Lean 4.31.0. Its Hill function has low- and high-concentration response levels. Setting those levels to 0 and 1 gives the Cells activation factor. Exchanging them gives inhibition. We compiled the original two monotonicity proofs on Lean 4.19.0 and proved that the Cells log-space expressions equal those two specializations for positive concentration and half-saturation. Activation increases or stays level and inhibition decreases or stays level for every positive real Hill exponent, including fractional ones. The optional check took 7.6 s with dependencies cached. The result applies to a scalar input of `species_regulated_availability@1` and `local_species_reactions@1`. The upstream authors give steady-state results for acyclic networks under additional assumptions and list the general oscillator result as open. [Upstream Hill definition](https://github.com/marpaia/grn-lean/blob/070ba8da6a09bdc4b0550396c61611668768bc0b/GRN/Dynamics/VectorField.lean#L36) · [Upstream monotonicity proofs](https://github.com/marpaia/grn-lean/blob/070ba8da6a09bdc4b0550396c61611668768bc0b/GRN/Dynamics/Sensor.lean#L30) · [Upstream circuit results and open questions](https://github.com/marpaia/grn-lean/blob/070ba8da6a09bdc4b0550396c61611668768bc0b/README.md) · [Cells specialization](checks/hill/Response.lean) · [Availability operator](../src/cells/operators/species_regulated_availability.py#L674)

On the existing reaction test grid we also compared the compiled CPU float64 factors with a 65-digit evaluation of the upstream formula on the same binary inputs. All 540 values agreed within 4.72 × 10⁻¹⁶ absolute error. The engine's existing reaction-rate test checks the same factors inside `resolved_pump_composite@1`. A measured concentration–response curve in a named preparation would be needed to test its choice of half-saturation or exponent. [Numerical comparison](checks/hill/compare.py) · [Run record](PROGRESS.md#19-november-2026--hill-activation-and-inhibition) · [Engine helper](../src/cells/laws/reactions/hill_factors.py#L28) · [Engine reaction-rate test](../tests/unit/test_hill_factors.py)

**Reaction networks.** In MIT-licensed `crnt-lean` on Lean 4.34.0, a weight orthogonal to every reaction vector has constant weighted concentration along a differentiable mass-action solution. Cells already proves conservation of a weighted *amount* across any finite signed reaction extents with balanced integer coefficients. That covers the numerical update directly and allows separate accounting for transport and boundary exchange. Mapping the upstream theorem to amounts also needs the compartment volumes. [Upstream trajectory theorem](https://github.com/marpaia/crnt-lean/blob/7386777488a41efd92e6abc2bccf889fb611660e/CRNT/Dynamics/ConservationLaw.lean#L46) · [Cells finite-update theorem](CellsProofs/Chemistry/Stoichiometry.lean#L27) · [Checked enzyme inventories](claims/enzyme-inventories.md)

The closed enzyme fixture in `resolved_pump_composite@1` has the graph `E + S ⇌ C → E + P`. Its two independent reaction vectors preserve total enzyme `E + C` and total substrate units `S + C + P`. It has three complexes, one connected component and stoichiometric rank two, giving deficiency 3 − 1 − 2 = 0. The catalytic step has no return path from `E + P`. This rules out applying the weakly reversible deficiency-zero theorem for a positive equilibrium. We checked these hypotheses against the Cells fixture and reran its compiled half-step. We reviewed the CRNT source, including its audit of the core theorems. Frontier conjectures are tracked separately. For a future equilibrium claim, use the upstream JSON analyzer to screen networks, then generate and check a per-network Lean certificate on the upstream pin. Only the latter goes through the Lean kernel for that network. [Cells fixture](../validation/tissue/species_reactions_fixtures.py#L248) · [Upstream theorem scope](https://github.com/marpaia/crnt-lean/blob/7386777488a41efd92e6abc2bccf889fb611660e/README.md) · [Core axiom audit](https://github.com/marpaia/crnt-lean/blob/7386777488a41efd92e6abc2bccf889fb611660e/test/AxiomAudit.lean) · [JSON analyzer contract](https://github.com/marpaia/crnt-lean/blob/7386777488a41efd92e6abc2bccf889fb611660e/docs/analyze-contract.md#trust-boundary) · [Certificate format](https://github.com/marpaia/crnt-lean/blob/7386777488a41efd92e6abc2bccf889fb611660e/docs/generated-certificates.md)

**Dimensional analysis.** The existing optional import from `LeanDimensionalAnalysis` passed again on the extracellular face law. It checks that amount/time times energy/amount has dimension energy/time. Our scalar theorem checks the separate factor of 0.001 when the engine multiplies fmol/s by J/mol to obtain pJ/s. This pattern is directly reusable for another transport ledger. [Upstream dimension algebra](https://github.com/ATOMSLab/LeanDimensionalAnalysis/tree/de263eed945693058ef2b8a1fa56c2ec5642ea7a/DimensionalAnalysis) · [Face-power proof](checks/units/FacePower.lean)

**Other suggestions.** LeanChemicalTheories already supplied the derivation behind our single-site binding proof. LeanLJ’s periodic particle-pair energies and LeanBET’s adsorption surface areas are more distant from our current laws. Their comparisons with numerical reference calculations are worth keeping as examples. SciLean is a candidate for a future differentiation or solver claim, and Physlib for a matching physical foundation. Mathlib already supplies the finite sums, orders, exponentials and logarithms in the default proofs. ## Where a proof fits in Cells

The existing registry already gives each proposed proof a law ID and a host model. Its `cells.declarations@2` manifest also records available units, signs, source locations and test references. The tissue model binds its declared structure to array kernels. In its explicit transport calculation it derives concentrations from amounts and volumes, multiplies junction availability by the gate, and calls the surface transport kernel. The latter computes the flux and assigns it to the owning cells. A proof can therefore refer to the declared law, the formula and the code that applies it. ## First experiment with a gap junction

1. **Prove the direction.** Let `J(a,b,v)` be the signed flux of one ion species from A to B in fmol/s, with concentrations `a,b` and voltage difference `v = φA − φB`. Use positive concentrations and temperature, the declared gate range and the engine’s voltage domain. Exchanging endpoints with the same route parameters should give `J(b,a,−v) = −J(a,b,v)`. When the potentials are equal and area, permeability and opening are positive, `a > b` must give positive flux. A backwards law or one that always returns zero can conserve total amount and still fail this directional check. [Flux kernel](../src/cells/laws/junctions/selective.py#L19) · [Zero-voltage branch](../src/cells/laws/electrochemistry.py#L17)
2. **Prove the accounting.** Each internal junction books its signed flux once with a minus at its sender and once with a plus at its receiver. On a finite graph with valid endpoints, summing the junction contributions over cells gives zero for each species, even when several junctions share a cell. Multiplying each flux by its valence, Faraday’s constant and the fmol-to-mol conversion gives the charge balance. Cells of different volumes can have different concentration changes while conserving amount. Extending the claim to a tissue exchanging material with a held bath requires the signed reservoir supply. [Owner updates and reservoir accounting](../src/cells/numerics/surface_transport.py#L310)
3. **Connect the theorem to an engine run.** Pin Lean 4 and Mathlib and have Lean check the exact statements. Exercise the existing gradient, reversed, blocked and padded fixtures against the independent numerical reference, then use the tissue model’s per-cell ledger check. Swapping the two bookings of a nonzero transfer in comparison data should fail the local check while preserving the global sum. Record the proof result and the numerical residual at the existing tolerance. Use the project’s snapshot tool to select the engine, fixture and proof sources together, including dirty or untracked proof inputs. The theorem covers the real-arithmetic law. The comparisons establish how closely the compiled engine follows it on those cases. [Surface tests](../tests/unit/test_surface_transport.py#L102) · [Independent reference](../validation/tissue/surface_reference.py#L71) · [Tissue ledger test](../tests/unit/test_surface_pump_v3_imex.py#L80) · [Snapshot tool](../validation/source_snapshot.py)

## Organizing the Cells proof library

| Primary home for a proof | Question it answers quickly | Cost as Cells grows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engine law or operator | What have we proved for `constant_field@1`, and what should we recheck when its code changes? | Easy to start from a code review. A scientist looking for all applications of a conservation principle has to follow several law IDs. |
| Biological mechanism, indexed by engine ID | What do we know about ion transport, and which Cells models use that result? | Works across model versions and scales. It requires a small mapping from each engine use to the relevant theorem. |
| Model or experiment | Which assumptions and results does this tissue experiment depend on? | Makes the case for one run easy to read. A membrane law used by several experiments needs a shared home as well. |

**I’d organize the Cells library by biological mechanism** in one Lean project under `lean/`. Give a model its own module when it combines mechanisms or adds assumptions. For the pilot, `CellsProofs/Transport/ConstantField.lean` would hold the equation and its direction theorem. `CellsProofs/Accounting/Transfer.lean` would hold the finite-graph balance. Add chemistry, mechanics or other subjects when a Cells model supplies their first question. [Lean modules](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Source-Files-and-Modules/) · [Cells flux kernel](../src/cells/laws/junctions/selective.py#L19)

A short index in this folder can answer the reverse question. For each claim, record its Lean theorem name, the versioned Cells law and host, and the existing numerical comparison. The declarations manifest supplies the engine identities. For example, the reversal theorem would map to `constant_field@1` on `resolved_pump_tissue@3` and the route-reversal test. After that proof lands, the entry could show that direction is proved and a floating-point error bound is still open. A new engine version can reuse the mathematical result and record its own comparison. Scientific qualification stays in its existing record, with links to biological measurements where available. [Declaration identities](../docs/library/DECLARATIONS_FORMAT.md#L72) · [Reversal test](../tests/unit/test_surface_transport.py#L141)

The first two mechanisms show how this organization would pay off. For a group of cells, transfers along its internal junctions cancel, leaving only flux across its boundary. For the enzyme fixture, binding and catalysis preserve total enzyme when bound enzyme is counted too. If a later model combines transport and reactions, and both preserve the same weighted inventory, the composed update preserves it. External supply enters as a boundary term. This gives the team a way to build a tissue-level result out of smaller proofs and to see exactly which assumptions it inherits. [Transport accounting](../src/cells/numerics/surface_transport.py#L316) · [Closed enzyme fixture](../validation/tissue/species_reactions_fixtures.py#L248)
