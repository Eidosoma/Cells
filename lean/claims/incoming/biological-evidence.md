## junction-flux-reversal

**Wang and Veenstra (1997), Figures 1A–E and 6, Table V.** Dual whole-cell recordings from rat Cx43-expressing N2A cell pairs resolve single-channel currents in pA. For the example pair in Figure 1, symmetric CsCl solutions and steps from a common holding potential give a channel current around −5.0 pA at −50 mV and levels around +4.8 to +4.95 pA at +50 mV. Figure 6 and Table V add asymmetric salt conditions and show how the voltage sign is tied to a chosen endpoint. Those reversal potentials are voltages at which summed electrical current crosses zero. [Wang and Veenstra, Journal of General Physiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9101407/)

**Lin and Veenstra (2007), Figure 1B–C and E–F.** Paired whole-cell recordings use opposite 70/140 and 140/70 mM KCl assignments and both voltage polarities in Cx40-expressing N2A pairs. The recordings include spermine on the same designated side and were designed to measure inhibition. Junctional current is reported relative to the zero-transjunctional-voltage baseline. The experiment addresses exchange of the salt gradient, with species-resolved flux and fixed opening still untested. Heterotypic Cx43/Cx45 recordings further show that conductance can change with voltage polarity. [Lin and Veenstra, Biophysical Journal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1896261/) · [Palacios-Prado and Bukauskas (2009), Figures 1 and 3–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736430/)

## junction-concentration-gradient

**Palacios-Prado and Bukauskas (2009), Figure 2B and Equations 1–3.** In doubly voltage-clamped cells from a human cell line expressing Cx43-EGFP, an anionic tracer (Alexa Fluor 350) passed from a loaded cell to its neighbor near zero junction voltage. The authors recorded fluorescence in both cells, corrected receiver accumulation for pipette loss and reported a mean single-channel permeability of about 83 × 10⁻¹⁵ cm³/s near zero voltage. The plotted flux is normalized and the concentration difference is represented by fluorescence. This is a matched precedent for direction of transfer of a charged tracer. [Palacios-Prado and Bukauskas, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736430/)

**Eitelmann et al. (2023), Figure 3 and §3.3.** Sodium-sensitive fluorescence in acute mouse brain slices tracked a Na⁺ increase in an electroporated astrocyte followed by smaller increases in neighboring astrocytes. Langer et al. independently found that a local sodium load spread to neighboring astrocytes and that this spread was strongly suppressed in Cx30/Cx43 double-deficient mice. The imaging measures concentration changes across a network, with individual junction voltage and Na⁺ amount per junction unresolved. [Eitelmann et al., Frontiers in Cellular Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncel.2023.1151608) · [Langer et al. (2012), Glia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22025386/)

**Musa et al., Figure 11A–C, G–H.** Wild-type rat Cx40 pairs with 70/140 mM KCl yield a single-channel current–voltage curve with an offset-corrected reversal at −14.4 mV. The asymmetric-salt curve can constrain the sign of total diffusion current near zero voltage. The reported K⁺/Cl⁻ partition depends on the GHK relation, so it provides context for a species model but cannot serve as an independent species-flux reference for that same relation. [Musa et al., Journal of Physiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16284078/)

**Zonta et al. (2013), Figure 1A–C and Methods on dual whole-cell patch clamp and fluorescence imaging.** The source and receiver traces both quantify the same charged tracer, calcein, in pairs from a human cell line expressing hCx26 (three pairs, mean ± SEM). The authors estimate recipient volume by optical sectioning and infer open channel count from junctional conductance after establishing the second whole-cell recording. Permeability is `(3.0 ± 1.0) × 10⁻³ μm³/s` per channel, derived over the window marked `t1–t2` in panel C. At the authors’ reference gradient of 125 μM this gives `226 ± 75 molecules/s/channel`, or `(3.75 ± 1.25) × 10⁻⁷ fmol/s/channel`, by unit conversion. Panel A tracks the two changing cellular concentrations. Only the donor was in whole-cell configuration during the transfer interval used in the estimate, so equal endpoint voltage was not independently imposed in that interval. Calcein provides a calibrated tracer precedent. The available records still leave the same-species inorganic-ion rate at two clamped voltages unresolved. [Zonta et al., Cell Communication and Signaling](https://doi.org/10.1186/1478-811X-11-15) · [Full text and Figure 1](https://d-nb.info/1108270298/34)

## junction-blocking

**Zhao Wang et al. (2010), Figure 2E–F (PMID 20089928).** In a dual-clamped native bovine pigmented/nonpigmented ciliary epithelial cell pair, the junctional current response disappeared after 2.5 minutes in 4.3 mM heptanol (2E) and returned after 7.5 minutes of washout (2F). Figure 6 reports reduced Lucifer Yellow transfer in parallel experiments. The measured current trace supports reversible electrical suppression at the tested voltage steps. The paper gives no numerical residual-current detection limit or species-specific fmol/s bound for this experiment. [Wang et al., American Journal of Physiology—Cell Physiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20089928/)

Tracer exclusion can occur during electrical coupling. In a voltage-gated heterotypic junction the Alexa Fluor 350 flux estimate reached zero while about 5 nS of junctional conductance persisted. Use the current trace to characterize electrical block and the tracer trace to characterize that tracer. [Palacios-Prado and Bukauskas (2009), Figure 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736430/)

**Levin and Mercola (1998), Figure 1.** With dorsal tracer injection, the paper reports Lucifer Yellow transfer in 41% of control *Xenopus* embryos (133 embryos) and 5% after heptanol exposure (43 embryos). These are fractions of embryos scored positive, without a flux rate or detection limit. Landesman et al. (2000) found that light scatter and differential pigmentation could mimic Lucifer Yellow transfer in whole mounts. Their fixed sections showed no Lucifer Yellow transfer under their conditions, whereas neurobiotin transfer was detected. The tracer result therefore needs the assay caveat alongside it. [Levin and Mercola, Developmental Biology](https://drmichaellevin.org/publications/documents/1998DevBiol.pdf) · [Landesman et al., Journal of Cell Biology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10953017/)

**Emmons-Bell et al. (2015), Figures 3 and 6.** The Levin-coauthored planarian study measures head anatomy and relative voltage patterns after octanol exposure. These endpoints connect the perturbation to a tissue phenotype. They leave the magnitude of junctional ion flux during exposure unresolved. [Emmons-Bell et al., International Journal of Molecular Sciences](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/16/11/26065)

## extracellular-face-direction

**Weber et al. (2015), Figure 6A–B.** Single-channel recordings in epithelial monolayers show opposite shifts in the claudin-2 paracellular current reversal potential when 90% of NaCl is replaced on opposite bathing sides. This gives an extracellular-to-extracellular comparison with exchanged salt conditions. The reported zero is for net electrical current through a selective pore. Species-specific net flux and the zero-flux point of an individual ion remain unmeasured. [Weber et al., eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/09906)

**Saghyan et al. (2012), Figures 1C–D and 2.** A selective electrode measured time-dependent extracellular tetramethylammonium concentration after point-source release in rat hippocampal slices. This supplies concentration traces for spatial diffusion. The experiment does not measure a species flux together with the voltage drop across a specified interface. The Levin-coauthored proton study below measures extracellular flux density near a tissue surface, with H⁺ supplied by membrane transport. [Saghyan et al., Journal of Neuroscience Methods](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22230768/)

## extracellular-face-dissipation

**Galvez et al. (2008), Figure 3 and Tables 1–2.** Cultured trout gill epithelia provide a precedent for measuring both directions of ²²Na⁺ transfer and the transepithelial voltage in the same preparation. The authors estimate bath Na⁺ activities and use them with the voltage to predict the passive flux ratio. The measured ratios show an active transport contribution, which limits use of the net flux as a passive-face benchmark. [Galvez et al., In Vitro Cellular & Developmental Biology—Animal](https://www.zoology.ubc.ca/~woodcm/Woodblog/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/Galvez-et-al.-2008-In-Vitro-Cell.pdf)

A direct comparison needs species-resolved net flux and independently determined activities and voltage across the same passive interface. The product of the signed flux in fmol/s and the potential drop in J/mol, multiplied by 10⁻³, gives pJ/s. The studies above do not supply that passive-interface power measurement.

## transfer-orientation

**Wang and Veenstra (1997), Figure 1A and C.** The simultaneously recorded junctional components of the two whole-cell currents have equal amplitudes and opposite polarities as the channel opens and closes. Integrating the paired junctional components over the same window would give opposing charge transfers in C. This is an empirical analogue of paired debit and credit. The experiment measures clamp currents, so it cannot supply the net amount of one ion gained or lost by each cell over that window. [Wang and Veenstra, Journal of General Physiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9101407/)

## transfer-inventory

**Kuki et al. (1990), Figures 3–4.** In isolated perfused rat hearts, ³⁹K NMR tracked relative intracellular K⁺ depletion during metabolic inhibition and repletion during recovery. In separate preparations, coronary effluent K⁺ and flow yielded integrated net K⁺ loss. At 40 minutes, cumulative effluent loss was 139 ± 12 μmol/g dry weight (mean ± SEM, five hearts). The reference intracellular content by flame photometry was 332 ± 10 μmol/g dry weight (six hearts). Figure 4 compares percent effluent loss with percent decrease in the NMR signal and reports slope 1.02, intercept 2.7 percentage points and R² = 0.95. [Kuki et al., Circulation Research, full text](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.RES.67.2.401)

The separate assays support organ-scale agreement in K⁺ accounting across the perturbation. The tissue signal is specifically intracellular, the effluent crosses an organ boundary and extracellular storage can affect their relation. The plotted comparison cannot yield a same-heart closure residual or a whole-system charge balance. [Kuki et al., Methods and Discussion](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/01.RES.67.2.401)

**Adams et al. (2006), self-referencing ion-selective probe methods and Results on asymmetric H⁺ flux.** In this Levin-coauthored study, local H⁺ efflux measured in 15 *Xenopus* embryos averaged 4.1 ± 0.48 pmol cm⁻² s⁻¹ at the right ventral quadrant and 1.9 ± 0.29 at the left, between the four-cell stage and stage 6. A separate concanamycin test reduced the right-to-left efflux ratio from 2.1 ± 0.2 to 1.1 ± 0.05 (three embryos). These local flux densities constrain the direction and magnitude of surface transport. Regional balance would require flux integrated across the boundary and an inventory measurement over the same interval. [Adams et al., Development](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3136117/)

## reaction-inventory

**Eicher et al. (2012), Figure 5 and `supp_data.ods`, sheet `PGI`, upper block.** Quantitative ³¹P NMR gives glucose-6-phosphate (G6P) and fructose-6-phosphate (F6P) separately in five phosphoglucose isomerase courses from *E. coli* K-12 extracts. Across the first and last measured rows, the soluble G6P + F6P endpoint changes are −0.613357, −1.534898, +0.099302, +0.391083 and −0.207509 mM. The row-level values precede the spline fits. Figure 4A reports a separate illustrated acquisition and rounded concentrations. The supplement contains one populated raw FID folder, with timing and volume details that differ from the generic Figure 5 protocol. Common-standard calibration errors, bound species and unobserved pools limit interpretation of the residuals. [PGI worksheet and acquisition audit](pgi-evidence.md) · [Eicher et al., Metabolites](https://www.mdpi.com/2218-1989/2/4/818)

The named inventory can be one hexose-phosphate moiety per molecule. A quantitative comparison should sum the independently measured concentrations before imposing balances and use sample volume to convert to amount. The extract may contain bound or unobserved pools. Estimate their contribution and retain the residual rather than forcing the soluble sum to close.

## enzyme-inventories

**Rozovsky and McDermott (2007), Figures 3–5.** Solid- and solution-state NMR resolve ligand states of triosephosphate isomerase. In Figure 3 the major bound species is keto-dihydroxyacetone phosphate. Bound hydrated glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate is about 5–10% of that peak's population, depending on preparation. The authors give a detection limit of about 1% of the major species for that solid-state measurement. The experiment illustrates how to distinguish bound species and reports relative populations in selected conditions. Free enzyme, free substrate, the full bound pool and free product were not all independently quantified across the same active turnover interval. Side products and hydrated states matter when mapping this system onto the four-species claim. [Rozovsky and McDermott, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1794347/)

**Wilson and Konermann (2004), Scheme 1 and Figures 1–3.** Time-resolved mass spectrometry tracks a falling unmodified α-chymotrypsin signal alongside a rising acetyl-enzyme signal during hydrolysis of *p*-nitrophenyl acetate. The traces report counts per second. The unmodified signal cannot separate free enzyme from the initial noncovalent complex, and the chemistry releases two products in distinct steps. This supplies dynamic evidence for changing enzyme pools. A four-species amount balance still requires independent measurements of all four pools in one preparation. [Wilson and Konermann, Analytical Chemistry, full text](https://www.derekwilsonlab.ca/userfiles/files/TRMS_Applied_to_Enzymes.pdf)

## single-site-binding

**Rossi-Fanelli and Antonini (1958), Figure 2, Table I and the Methods sections “Oxygenation of Mb” and “Spectrophotometry.”** Purified human skeletal-muscle myoglobin in 50 mM Tris, pH 7.45, at 0.20 mM myoglobin was equilibrated with O₂ in a tonometer. Pressure was calculated from introduced gas and the tonometer conditions, with a correction for bound oxygen where relevant. Occupancy was observed by absorbance normalized against fully deoxygenated and fully oxygenated samples and averaged across wavelengths. Figure 2 plots unitless fractional oxygen saturation against O₂ partial pressure in mmHg for several temperatures. The 10 °C Table I `log₁₀ K = +0.49` gives `P50 = 0.324 Torr`. The mean Hill exponent across about 50 curves was 1.03, with reported range 0.95–1.1. That range concerns whole-curve fit shape across conditions. Pointwise pressure and occupancy errors and the replicate design for the 10 °C points are unavailable in the paper. [Rossi-Fanelli and Antonini, PMID 13584010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13584010/) · [Original article text and figure captions](https://doczz.net/doc/7948867/rossi-fanelli--a.-and-e.-antonini--1958---studies-on-the-...)

The crystallized human preparation contains three electrophoretic components. The authors separately characterize isolated Mb I and Mb II in Table IV at pH 8 and 20–40 °C, with Hill exponent 1 for each. Table IV provides component-level affinity evidence under its own conditions. The 10 °C Figure 2 data describe the preparation as assayed. [Rossi-Fanelli and Antonini, Table IV](https://doczz.net/doc/7948867/rossi-fanelli--a.-and-e.-antonini--1958---studies-on-the-...)

**Concentration basis.** At fixed temperature, assume gas–liquid equilibrium and dilute-solution Henry behavior, with free molar O₂ proportional to its partial pressure. A pure-water estimate at 10 °C is `α ≈ 2.24 μM/Torr`, from 11.29 mg O₂/L at 760 mmHg in water-saturated air, dry-air O₂ mole fraction 0.20946, vapor pressure about 9.21 Torr and molar mass 31.998 g/mol. This gives `K_d,app ≈ 0.73 μM` for the Table I pressure fit. The Tris solution’s exact solubility was not measured. Any constant α cancels from the occupancy prediction when both the free O₂ concentration and affinity are converted with the same α. The mass of O₂ bound to myoglobin is excluded from the free concentration. [Benson and Krause (1984)](https://doi.org/10.4319/lo.1984.29.3.0620) · [USGS freshwater table](https://pubs.usgs.gov/tm/09/a6.2/tm9a6.2.pdf)

**Retrospective fit and evaluation example.** The five points below are the first five 10 °C marker readings published in Caltech’s digitization of Figure 2. Fit only the two rows labeled Fit by unweighted least squares in occupancy space with the single-site curve `pO₂ / (pO₂ + P50)`. This gives `P50 = 0.331 Torr` and nominal `K_d,app = 0.743 μM`. The other three rows give a descriptive occupancy RMSE of 0.0422. The split is within one figure and retrospective. The authors’ Table I fit has no reported holdout split and can be used as context. The figure reading precision and observations’ experimental uncertainties are unknown, so the residuals cannot establish acceptance. [Caltech figure digitization, first five data-frame rows](https://bi1.caltech.edu/2017/code/t05_nonlinear_regression.html)

**Schenkman et al. (1997), Table 1 and Methods.** Equine myoglobin equilibrated against 16 known O₂/N₂ mixtures provides a separate primary assay. Multiwavelength spectroscopy estimated deoxy-, oxy- and metmyoglobin, allowing correction for inactive metmyoglobin. The reported fit gives `P50 = 2.39 Torr` at pH 7.0 and 37 °C. This estimate supplies context for the technique and temperature dependence. Species and assay conditions differ from the 10 °C human titration. [Schenkman et al., Journal of Applied Physiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9029202/)

**Helbo and Fago (2011), Figure 2A–B.** Purified rainbow trout myoglobin gives a separate primary O₂ equilibrium titration with reported affinity uncertainty. Panel A shows a representative occupancy curve at pH 8.3 and 25 °C. The paper reports `P50 = 4.92 ± 0.29 mmHg` at 25 °C, and panel B reports means ± SD across two or three determinations for its condition groups. This uncertainty applies to fitted affinities. A fit and evaluation split would still require digitizing experimental occupancy markers separately from fitted lines and establishing pointwise errors. This preparation has its own affinity and solution conditions. [Helbo and Fago, PMID 20962203](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20962203/) · [Original article including Figure 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47509922_Allosteric_modulation_by_S-nitrosation_in_the_low-O-2_affinity_myoglobin_from_rainbow_trout)

## regional-reaction-transport

**Fausto (1972), Figure 1A–D and Results under “Metabolism of orotic acid in perfused livers.”** In isolated rat livers supplied with [6-¹⁴C]orotic acid, samples track label in recirculating perfusate, acid-soluble liver extracts, bile and trapped CO₂. At 40 minutes the acid-soluble liver fraction contained roughly 80–90% of the supplied activity. Figure 1 presents means and SEM from four experiments. A suitable inventory is labeled carbon across chemical forms, including any products leaving via bile or CO₂. [Fausto, Biochemical Journal, figures and methods](https://portlandpress.com/biochemj/article-pdf/129/4/811/778954/bj1290811.pdf)

The study supplies tissue retention and evidence for multiple routes across the boundary. The figure mixes activity concentrations, rates or interval collections, and fractions of the input. Reconstructing a balance at a common time would require the corresponding volumes and tissue masses, corrections for sampling, total activity carried by outgoing pools and an accounting for tissue fractions outside the acid-soluble assay. The preparation is evidence for reaction coupled to transport at organ scale. The numerical split step belongs to the engine comparison.

## rptec-voltage-c3

Michael Levin coauthored both RPTEC studies below. [Bonzanni et al. author list](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31852666/) · [Adelfio et al. author list](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35157435/)

**Bonzanni et al. (2020), Figures 2A–B and 3C, Electrophysiology methods.** The exact RPTEC/TERT1 endpoint is the resting whole-cell voltage under C3 at 25 °C with zero applied current. C3 has 65.4 mM K⁺ and 80 mM Na⁺. Figure 3C labels means ± SEM and gives 20 cells across three experiments for C3. The Lean comparison fits C1/C2 and evaluates C3 retrospectively. Its curated C3 mean is approximately −18 mV, the prediction is −14.59 mV and the signed residual is +3.41 mV. The ±2 mV curation bound describes figure reading. The search located no additional public cell- or experiment-level C1–C3 data; the repository’s supplement review also found no such table or recordings. [Bonzanni et al., Biology Open](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31852666/) · [Curated endpoints](../../../data/biology/bonzanni2020_rptec_v1/voltage_endpoints.csv) · [BIO-003 closeout](../../../reports/BIO003_PILOT_CLOSEOUT.md)

**Adelfio et al. (2022), Figure 1D and Patch Clamp methods.** This related study uses RPTEC/TERT1 and measures a blocker-sensitive current reversal potential under a different bath containing 70 mM KCl. The reported −13.8 ± 3.14 mV concerns the reversal potential of that current component. It gives context for a K⁺ conductance in this cell line. The C3 resting-voltage uncertainty remains unresolved. [Adelfio et al., ACS Biomaterials Science & Engineering](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9906498/)

## Comparing measurements with claim units

- For a current carried by one identified ion of valence `z`, `1 pA = 0.01036 / |z| fmol/s` in amount-rate magnitude. Apply the direction convention separately. A mixed-ion current measures net charge transfer and cannot resolve individual ion rates without additional constraints.
- Convert concentrations using the corresponding compartment volumes. For example, `1 mM × 1 pL = 1 fmol`. For a tissue or perfusate comparison use the study’s mass and volume bases before comparing changes. For radiotracer results, retain radioactivity or fraction of injected activity until specific activity and the target label inventory are established.
