# Biological evidence for `cells.junction_voltage_relaxation@1`

Sahu and Bera (2013) used dual whole-cell voltage clamp on N2a cell pairs expressing untagged wild-type Cx43 on both sides of the junction. They held one cell at 0 mV and stepped the other from −120 to +120 mV in 20 mV increments. Each step lasted 20 s, with 30 s of recovery at 0 mV and a 100 ms, +10 mV prepulse for normalization. Figure 1 shows representative junctional currents and normalized steady conductance at both polarities. Table 2 lists `n = 7` for wild-type Cx43. Values are means ± SEM. The residual conductances are the fitted high-voltage plateaus from the two polarity branches. At a separate fixed-voltage comparison, the wild-type current decay had two fitted time constants, **70 ± 5 ms** and **931 ± 54 ms**. These are the fast and slow components of the same decay. The Methods and Results specify a −100 mV junctional step for the kinetics. The Figure S2 caption gives 100 mV without the minus sign. [Publisher PDF, Table 2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0060506&type=printable) · [PLOS One, Figure S2](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0060506.s002)

- **Residual conductance and polarity.** Both voltage branches retained roughly a third of the prepulse conductance at high voltage. The similar plateaus and midpoint magnitudes support approximate polarity symmetry of the steady response in this preparation. A test of exact equality of the trajectories would require matched starting states and fitted decays at both polarities.
- **Single-exponential relaxation.** At fixed junctional voltage, conductance is junctional current divided by that constant voltage, so the two time constants also describe the conductance decay. The two fitted components limit the one-state law to an approximation for this wild-type preparation. The paper gives no polarity-paired estimates of the two time constants.
- **Availability.** The voltage-clamp series did not independently vary or measure an availability factor while holding intrinsic gating fixed. The product `gate × availability` remains untested by this comparison.
