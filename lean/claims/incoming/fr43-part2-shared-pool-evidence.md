# Finite pools spent and replenished by separate processes

**FR-43 part 2.** Huber et al. provide the closest fixed-volume comparison. Their cell-free cascade measures a UTP pool, its upstream uridine-bearing intermediates, and the product of UTP consumption in the same time course, with public data and uncertainties for a separate validation run. Skok et al. provide a changing-volume comparison with an externally metered ATP feed and accumulation of an independently quantified RNA product. Eicher et al. Figure 9B gives a partial comparison using a reused enzyme-kinetics series.

## UTP synthesis and consumption at fixed volume

Huber et al. (2026), Figures 2 and 8 and Methods §§4.2–4.3. Polyphosphate kinase supplies UTP from UDP, and a different enzyme, GlmU, consumes UTP to produce UDP-GalNAc with one uridine moiety per product. HPAEC–UV separately resolves uridine (Uri), UMP, UDP, UTP and UDP-GalNAc in sampled aliquots. The methods give a prepared batch volume of **500 µL**. The study reports no measured volume trajectory. In its comparison, one mM corresponds to 0.5 µmol at the prepared volume. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mec.2026.e00287)

The Figure 8 titer validation data are the repository's `process_optimization/exp_val_data/UDP-GalNAc40_TO7h_with_initConcColumns.txt` and matching `_SD.txt`. Selected means ± reported SD below use mM throughout. The 7 h product replicates are **34.99609554, 35.09782038 and 41.74564590 mM**. The archived SD of 3.158065 mM equals the population SD of these three values. ## ATP feed and RNA production at changing volume

Skok et al. (2022), Figure 4A/C and Methods §§2.2–2.3. The reactor run starts at **100 mL** and a nominal **8 mM ATP**. The three delivered feed volumes are **21.0, 25.4 and 30.8 mL**, for **177.2 mL** reconstructed before sampling withdrawals. The methods report **25 µL** withdrawals every 15–30 min and Figure 4C plots the volume profile. The paper does not specify a separate volume sensor. The methods give **23 mM of each NTP** in the feed stock, while the Figure 3 caption describes **4 mM of each NTP**. Figure 4C also labels the first phase as **40 mL**, where the methods give **40 mL/h** and **21 mL** delivered. The source discrepancies matter for a quantitative amount balance. [Primary paper, Table 1 and Figures 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1002/cite.202200133)

Figure 4A resolves ATP, GTP and eGFP RNA by chromatography. RNA has its own calibration using purified eGFP RNA. The accompanying Stover et al. processed file `skokcontinuousfeeding.csv` lists, at its own time labels **0.75, 1.00 and 6.25 h**, ATP **0.875000, 2.968750 and 2.468750 mM** and RNA **13.669040, 19.198090 and 38.089011 µM**. The CSV has no per-sample volume trajectory. Its early labels appear offset from the original reactor timeline, so a volume join needs reconciliation with Figure 4A/C. The generic SD column contains the value 1 for RNA and NTP rows. The original reactor report supplies no pointwise replicate uncertainty for those traces. [Primary analytics and Figures 4–5](https://doi.org/10.1002/cite.202200133) · [Processed table](https://github.com/nathanmstover/fedbatchIVT/blob/0ab946df31b901b0b2a49779c5afdfd74f95a5d1/data/external/skok/skokcontinuousfeeding.csv) · [Stover et al., Figure 2d](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbic.202500485)

The authors planned the reactor feed using the **separate 100 µL shaker experiment**. Its Figure 2 and Table 2 give ATP **8.00 to 0.37 mM over 0–60 min**, then **4.16 to 0.85 mM over 60–120 min**, with the values after feeding taken at the start of each phase. Table 2's consumption rates are computed from ATP depletion. For an independent check, use the measured RNA accumulation and its base composition for productive consumption, the delivered feed and volume as inputs, and hold out the reactor ATP trace. The companion CSV gives **315 A residues per transcript**. Abortive transcripts and ATP hydrolysis can contribute additional spending, and the fed-batch paper does not quantify those routes over time. Feed mass and sampling times need reconciliation before an absolute held-out balance. The volume change is externally supplied. [Primary paper, Table 2 and Figures 2, 4 and 6](https://doi.org/10.1002/cite.202200133) · [Companion thermal shaker data](https://github.com/nathanmstover/fedbatchIVT/blob/0ab946df31b901b0b2a49779c5afdfd74f95a5d1/data/external/skok/skokdiscretefeeding.csv)

## Eicher Figure 9B

Eicher et al. (2012), Figure 9B and §2.3, model the same extract time course as Figure 3, top left. PGI exchanges F6P with G6P and PFK spends F6P and ATP to form FBP and ADP. Figure 9B initializes from measured concentrations and uses fitted PGI/PFK kinetics. The **0–30 min** segment is a time window excluded from fitting within the same run. Subsequent points share the fit. F6P, the shared pool, is quantified only at initialization in this panel. The model includes an assumed first-order hydrolysis term. AMP and phosphate are absent from the panel. [Primary paper, Figures 3 and 9 and §§2.1.4, 2.2.2, 2.3](https://www.mdpi.com/2218-1989/2/4/818)

The publisher's `supp_data.ods`, `PFK` sheet, `A4:G86` (Dataset 1), contains 83 processed measurements on the nominal 1.66 min grid before the spline block (row 89). The PEP column is zero throughout this dataset. The §3.3 standard assay volume is **1 mL** at 25 °C. Per-point uncertainty is unavailable. The independent coupled assay gives a PFK maximum forward rate of **0.4435 ± 0.0001 µmol min⁻¹ mg⁻¹ protein** (§2.2.2), measured under its own assay conditions. Neither it nor the kinetics in Figure 9B supplies the actual gross reaction rates in the NMR tube. Dataset 5 (`AG:AM`) has identical FBP and ADP entries in rows 4–42, so these columns cannot establish independent product measurements. Dataset 6 (`AO:AU`, rows 4–37) records PEP **3.994577 to 2.027149 mM** and ATP **2.745764 to 1.949247 mM** over **1.66–56.44 min**. The authors observed additional PEP metabolism. PEP disappearance therefore combines several routes and cannot quantify ATP replenishment by itself.
