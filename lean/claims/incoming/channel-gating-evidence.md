# Voltage-clamp evidence for the tissue channel laws

At fixed voltage and ion concentrations, normalized Cells current follows the law’s full open fraction. The comparisons below evolve both gates as `x(t) = x∞ + (x(0) − x∞) exp(−t/τx)` on each clamp step, starting at holding-voltage equilibrium. A fit to whole-current decay measures a different quantity from an individual gate’s relaxation time. [Runnable clamp calculation](../../checks/channel_clamp_evidence.py)

## Kv1.1–Kv1.6

**Kv1.1.** Ranjan et al. (2019) recorded rat homomeric Kv1.1 in CHO cells by automated whole-cell clamp at 25 °C. Figure 2C gives **313.25 ± 81.7 ms** at −20 mV (mean ± SD, 67 cells for the inactivation panel), from a single-exponential fit to current from its peak to the end of the 1.5 s conditioning pulse. Applying the same fit with a free offset to the Cells `m h²` current gives **about 8 s**, with current falling to 88% of its peak by pulse end. This calculation starts at −80 mV equilibrium and omits the protocol’s 10 ms step to −90 mV. Starting equilibrated at −90 or −70 mV gives 7.9–8.2 s. The fitted model time constant extends beyond the 1.5 s pulse. Its value depends on the fit window and offset. The residual current directly shows how little the model decays during the pulse. The paper also reports a rise fit of **4.68 ± 1.76 ms** at −10 mV (mean ± SD, 71 cells for the activation panel) and a recovery fit of **0.85 s** at −80 mV (median, IQR 0.72–0.95 s, `n = 59`), after a 1.5 s conditioning pulse at +50 mV. The main text gives 150 ms for the recovery test pulse and Data Sheet 1 gives 200 ms. [Ranjan et al., Figure 2](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncel.2019.00358/full) · [Voltage protocols and fit equations](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/articles/450839/file/Data_Sheet_1.pdf/450839_supplementary-materials_datasheets_1_pdf/4)

**Kv1.4.** Stühmer et al. (1989) measured rat RCK4 in cell-attached oocyte patches. After a step from −80 to 0 mV, less than **2% of peak current** remained at 3.2 s (`n = 10`). Cells gives **0.57%**. The trace and accompanying text establish the direction of this ratio despite the reversed wording of Table I’s footnote g. Four fitted gate parameters in that table equal the Cells values. The activation midpoint and slope are −21.7 and −16.9 mV, and the inactivation midpoint and slope are −73.6 and +12.8 mV (`n = 5` for each). This overlap limits the endpoint’s independence as a biological test. The study does not specify a recording temperature for these values. [Stühmer et al., Table I and Fig. 6](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1460-2075.1989.tb08483.x)

In a second preparation, Roeper et al. (1997) recorded rat Kv1.4 expressed in HEK293 cells at 30 ± 0.5 °C. On a −80 to +40 mV step, the whole-current fit gave decay components of **16.6 ± 0.9 ms** and **43.3 ± 2.3 ms** (mean ± SEM, `n = 12`, with 73.5% and 26.5% of the fitted amplitudes). Cells uses one inactivation gate with a 119 ms time constant and retains **21.0%** of peak current at 200 ms on that step. The published values describe a joint fit with activation and two decay components. Comparing the endpoint quantitatively would require a measured fraction at +40 mV. [Roeper et al., Fig. 1 and Results](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.17-10-03379.1997) · [Reproduce both Cells pulses](../../checks/channel_clamp_evidence.py)

**Kv1.2, Kv1.3, Kv1.5 and Kv1.6.** Ranjan et al.’s 25 °C rat CHO recordings cover each of these isoforms in Figure 3A. Kv1.3 and Kv1.5 show substantial cell-to-cell differences, and Kv1.3 can show delayed onset of inactivation. Kv1.6 inactivates at 25 °C and is nearly non-inactivating at 15 °C. Independent Kv1.2 recordings show fast and slow activation modes and switching after a depolarizing prepulse. Quantitative protocol matching for these four isoforms remains open. [Ranjan et al., Figures 3 and 6 and Figures S7 and S11](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncel.2019.00358/full) · [Rezazadeh et al., Figures 1–3](https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.107.116160)

## NaV1.2

Begemann et al. (2019) expressed adult human wild-type NaV1.2 with β1 and β2 in HEK293T cells and recorded whole-cell currents at room temperature. On the 10 ms step from −120 to 0 mV, the current’s **10–90% rise was 0.38 ± 0.05 ms** and the fast component of a two-exponential decay fit was **0.58 ± 0.07 ms**. A 500 ms conditioning step followed by a +5 mV test gave a steady-state inactivation midpoint of **−63.53 ± 0.79 mV**. Paired +5 mV steps with recovery at −90 mV gave **7.02 ± 0.86 ms** (all mean ± SEM, `n = 9` each). Under the same 0 mV step, the Cells `m³h` current gives **0.296 ms** for the 10–90% rise. Applying the 500 ms prepulses and taking the peak of each model +5 mV test gives half-availability near **−54.9 mV**, using the peak after a −120 mV prepulse for normalization. The published fit used prepulses down to −130 mV. The model uses its declared lower limit of −120 mV and the interpolated half-response. The model rises slightly faster and places inactivation about 9 mV more positive. Fits to the model’s whole-current decay and paired-pulse recovery remain open. [Begemann et al., Figure 2 and Table 2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10020-019-0073-6)

## Kir2.1 and the potassium and sodium leaks

- **Kir2.1.** In Xenopus oocyte patches at 20–24 °C, Xie et al. (2002) found that the current–voltage curve measured at the end of 15 ms steps became nearly linear about 5 min after excision into Mg²⁺- and polyamine-free solution. Adding 100 µM cytoplasmic spermine restored inward rectification. In excised COS7 patches held at −60 mV, unitary current magnitude fell from **2.00 ± 0.04 pA** to **1.40 ± 0.04 pA** at 100 µM spermine (`n = 4` patches, uncertainty as printed), with no significant change in open probability. The Cells voltage-only `m h²` law lacks blocker concentration and a blocker-dependent unitary conductance. A rectifying whole-current trace alone cannot identify the two modeled gate rates. [Xie et al., Figures 1–3](https://rupress.org/jgp/article-pdf/120/1/53/1217576/jgp120153.pdf)
- **K and Na leaks.** Cells sets the open fraction to one for each single-ion constant-field pathway. This defines a background permeability and still allows current to vary with the driving force. Actual background channels can respond to other variables. In cell-attached COS patches held at 0 mV, expressed TREK-1 activity increased **17.4 ± 3.8-fold** from 22 to 42 °C at atmospheric pressure (`n = 19`). In HEK293T whole-cell recordings the human NALCN complex carried voltage-dependent current and conducted K⁺ as well as Na⁺. The leak laws have no specified molecular isoform, kinetic parameters or preparation-specific permeability to compare. [Maingret et al., Figure 4](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/19.11.2483) · [Chua et al., Figures 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaz3154)

## Chloride leak

Ling et al. (1994) recorded individual apical channels in cell-attached patches of primary rabbit collecting-tubule principal cells at 37 °C. Lowering nominal chloride on the extracellular face of the patch from 145 to 15 mM with gluconate shifted reversal from near 0 to about **+45 mV** on the negative-pipette-voltage axis (11 and 4 patches respectively). Zero on that axis corresponds to the resting membrane potential. Exchanging the cations while retaining chloride gave no shift. The reported reversal endpoints are approximate and have no stated uncertainty. [Ling et al., Fig. 1B and Results](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI117037)

Holding intracellular chloride and temperature fixed, the Cells chloride-only pathway predicts a **+60.6 mV** shift. The calculation uses the two nominal pipette concentrations. The observed direction agrees. The channel also conducted bromide and showed regulation by PGE₂ and cAMP. Basal open probability in four single-channel patches was 0.43 ± 0.08. This preparation leaves the generic Cells leak’s permeability and always-open assumption uncalibrated.
